## Why tourists are nervous about onsen

Common worries:

- **Nudity** in a shared space
- **Tattoos** and rejection signs
- **Not knowing the order** (wash vs soak)
- **Language** on signs
- **Body image** and comparison

All normal. Thousands of first-timers succeed every week by following a short routine. Staff expect questions.

## What an onsen actually is

An **onsen** (温泉) is a bath fed by **geothermally heated natural spring water**. Legally, the water must meet mineral and temperature rules. Tourists use the word loosely for any **traditional hot bath experience**, including many ryokan baths.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Onsen** | Natural hot spring bath |
| **Rotenburo** | Outdoor bath |
| **Kashikiri-buro** | Private reserved bath (pay extra) |
| **Day-trip onsen** | Public bath without overnight stay |

You are there to **soak and relax**, not to scrub in the tub like a bathtub at home.

## Onsen vs sento explained

| | **Onsen** | **Sento** (銭湯) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Water** | Natural hot spring (regulated) | Heated tap water, often community bath |
| **Vibe** | Resort, ryokan, scenic towns | Neighborhood bath, local regulars |
| **Price** | Often bundled in stay or day fee | Low entry fee |
| **Etiquette** | Same washing rules | Same washing rules |

For tourists, **the process is identical**: wash at stations, then soak in pools. Sento are great in cities if you want practice before a ryokan night.

## The bathing process step-by-step

**Example first visit (day onsen):**

1. Pay at reception. Receive locker key or wristband.
2. Go to **men's or women's** changing room (color signs: blue/men, red/women is common).
3. Remove all clothing. Store in locker. Take only **small towel** and toiletries into bath area.
4. Enter washing zone. Sit at a low stool. **Wash entire body** thoroughly.
5. Rinse stool and area lightly when done.
6. Enter **hot pool** slowly. Place small towel on head or beside pool, not in water.
7. Rotate through pools if multiple (hot, warm, cold plunge optional).
8. Exit, shower again if you wish, return to locker room, dress.

Total time: many people soak **30-60 minutes** including washing.

<TipBox title="Etiquette quick tip">
  Think **shower first, bath second**. The big tub is for soaking only.
</TipBox>

## Washing before entering the bath

This is the rule that matters most.

| Step | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Sit** | Use provided stool, do not stand and splash |
| **Soap** | Full body, hair if needed |
| **Rinse** | Remove all soap before entering shared tub |
| **Repeat** | If you leave for toilet, wash again before re-entering |

<InfoBox title="Tourists overthink this">
  You do not need a perfect performance. You need **no soap in the shared water** and **no dirt from the street**. Locals notice effort, not speed.
</InfoBox>

## Nudity and privacy realities

- **Gender-separated baths** are standard (see below).
- **Nudity is required** in most traditional onsen (no swimsuit).
- Everyone is focused on relaxing, not judging bodies.
- **Small towel** covers strategic moments while walking; it does not go in the tub.
- If nudity is a hard no, book **private bath** or **kashikiri** room.

**Practical scenario:** You feel self-conscious for three minutes. By minute ten, you are watching steam and mountain view, not other guests.

## Tattoo rules explained honestly

Japan has historical stigma around tattoos. Policies vary sharply.

| Policy type | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **No tattoos** | Entry refused or cover required |
| **Cover allowed** | Skin-colored cover sheets sold or rented |
| **Tattoo-friendly** | Explicit welcome signs |
| **Private bath** | Bypasses shared rules |

**What to do:**

1. Check website or call: *tattoo arimasu* (I have a tattoo).
2. Search **tattoo OK onsen** in your city.
3. Book **private onsen** in ryokan if unsure.
4. Do not use bandages that peel off in water.

Rules are tightening at some chains and loosening at tourist areas. **Never assume** all onsen are the same.

## Gender-separated baths

| Sign | Usually means |
| --- | --- |
| 男 / 男性 | Men |
| 女 / 女性 | Women |
| 混浴 | Mixed bath (rare; ask rules) |

Some modern facilities offer **alternate hours** or **private family baths**. Ryokan may have small **family bath** reservation slots.

## Outdoor baths (rotenburo)

**Rotenburo** (露天風呂) are outdoor pools, often with views of mountains, snow, or gardens.

| Tip | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Hair tie** | Wind and photos |
| **Winter visits** | Cold air, hot water contrast is magical |
| **Quiet voice** | Sound carries outside |
| **Camera** | Never in bath area |

Snow-view onsen in Nagano or Hokkaido are bucket-list experiences. Summer rotenburo feel calmer at dawn.

## Ryokan onsen culture

Staying at a **ryokan** (traditional inn) often includes onsen access.

| Pattern | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Before dinner** | Popular soak time |
| **Morning bath** | Quiet, beautiful light |
| **Yukata** | Wear to and from bath; learn simple tie |
| **Multiple baths** | Some ryokan have two buildings or rotations |
| **Towels in room** | Room towel vs bath towel may differ; read card |

Lodging context: [where to stay in Japan](/guides/where-to-stay-japan).

**Example ryokan night:** Arrive 3 p.m., check in, soak before kaiseki dinner, second short morning soak, breakfast in yukata.

## What to bring

| Item | Need |
| --- | --- |
| **Toiletries** | Many places provide; bring if sensitive skin |
| **Small towel** | Often provided; confirm |
| **Hair tie** | Recommended |
| **Water bottle** | Hydrate after hot soak |
| **Makeup** | Remove before bath |
| **Contact lens case** | Steam bothers some wearers |
| **Plastic bag** | For wet items in locker |

Do not bring **phone, large towel in water, or jewelry** that tarnishes.

## Locker and changing room process

1. Remove shoes at entrance (follow shoe area rules).
2. Locker key on wrist band or pin.
3. **All clothes off** in changing room (underwear too).
4. Put valuables in locker; take key with you.
5. Use provided basket for clothes.
6. Sit on stools in changing area only if marked; do not block paths.

Toilet slippers in changing areas follow same rules as general Japan: [toilets and public etiquette](/guides/how-to-use-japanese-toilets).

## Common mistakes tourists make

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Jumping into tub without washing | Wash first, always |
| Towel in water | Keep towel out of tub |
| Soaking with soap on body | Rinse fully |
| **Photos** in bath | Never |
| Loud conversation | Quiet voice |
| Diving or swimming | Soak only |
| Wearing swimsuit in traditional onsen | Remove unless family pool says OK |
| Ignoring tattoo sign | Check policy first |
| Leaving locker key in pocket | Wrist band system |

## What Japanese people actually care about

Locals care about **hygiene and calm**, not your nationality.

| Matters | Does not matter much |
| --- | --- |
| Clean entry to tub | Perfect Japanese |
| Low noise | Body shape |
| Following shoe/slipper rules | First-timer nerves |
| Respecting tattoo policy | Brief awkward pause |

A quick *sumimasen* if you splash someone goes a long way.

## Hygiene and cleanliness expectations

- Pools are **filtered and circulated**; your soap would ruin that.
- Staff clean surfaces daily.
- If you have **open wound or contagious illness**, skip public bath.
- **Menstruation:** personal choice; some use tampons and soak; others prefer private bath. No universal ban; comfort first.
- Rinse sweat before re-entering if you used sauna.

## FAQ

### Do I have to be naked?

In most traditional gender-separated onsen, yes. Private baths allow swimsuits or privacy depending on facility.

### Can couples bathe together?

Some ryokan offer **private family baths** by reservation. Public mixed baths are uncommon.

### How hot is the water?

Often **40-42°C** in main tubs. Warm pools are milder. Enter slowly.

### How long should I soak?

Start **5-10 minutes** per tub. Exit if dizzy. Cool water pool optional between heats.

### Are tattoos always banned?

No. Policies vary. Tattoo-friendly onsen exist. Private baths are the safe fallback.

### Can I drink alcohol in the bath?

No in bath area. Some ryokan serve beer after bathing in lounge.

### What if I feel faint?

Exit slowly, sit, drink water. Heat and jet lag combine easily.

### Is onsen safe during pregnancy?

Ask your doctor. Many facilities advise against hot soaking.

### Do I tip at onsen?

No tipping. Pay posted fee or included ryokan rate.

### Can I wear glasses in the steam?

Risk of fog and fall. Contacts or go without if safe.
