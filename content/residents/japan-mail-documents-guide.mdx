## Why Japanese mail feels intimidating to foreign residents

| Fear | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **Kanji on envelope** | Often a logo plus a few key words |
| **Red stamps** | Official mark, not automatic fine |
| **Registered mail** | Common, not only legal trouble |
| **Silence after mistake** | Deadlines still pass |
| **Throwing away "junk"** | May be tax or visa related |

Japan still uses **paper for high-trust notices**. Digital apps exist, but your mailbox remains the official channel for many systems.

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Assuming everything important is email or LINE. Many institutions **mail first**, especially government.

**Scenario:** Month two in Japan. You find a thick envelope with your ward logo. You stash it unopened. Week three you learn it was **resident tax installment schedule**. Open mail within 48 hours until you know the patterns.

You do not need fluent Japanese on day one. Photograph envelope front, use translation app, ask ward **foreign resident desk** if your city has one.

## Resident tax and payment letters

**住民税 (juminzei)** often arrives in **installment slips** after your first full calendar year working in Japan.

| Item | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Notice letter** | Explains annual amount |
| **Payment slips** | Konbini or bank pay |
| **Deadline boxes** | Usually four installments |
| **Employer withholding** | May reduce paper if salaried |

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Thinking resident tax is paid only at year-end like some countries. Japan often uses **monthly or quarterly slips** mailed to your home.

Full guide: [Japanese taxes for foreign residents](/residents/japan-taxes-guide).

| Action | When |
| --- | --- |
| Open envelope immediately | First tax season |
| Match name and address | Typo risks |
| Pay by deadline | Late fees apply |
| Keep copies one year | Visa renewal, disputes |

## Registered mail and signatures

**特定記録郵便** and **本人限定受取郵便** require **ID and signature**.

| When used | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **My Number card delivery** | PIN letter separate |
| **Bank cash card** | First issue |
| **Residence card renewal pickup notice** | Pickup at office, not always mail |
| **Legal documents** | Contracts |

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Pink/redelivery notice in mailbox | You missed delivery |
| Bring ID | Residence card or passport plus card |
| Sign screen or paper | Name match matters |
| If away | Use redelivery scheduling |

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Ignoring pink slips. **Registered mail is not optional pickup.** Items return to sender after hold period.

Post basics: [Japan Post office guide](/guides/japan-post-office-guide).

## Forwarding mail after moving

When you move, file **転居届 (tenkyo todoke)** at post office or online.

| Service | Duration |
| --- | --- |
| **Standard forwarding** | Usually one year |
| **Per-item fee** | Some mail types |
| **Package forwarding** | Separate carrier rules |

| Move checklist | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Submit forwarding | Within 14 days ideal |
| Update ward office address | Residence registration |
| Update bank and utilities | Online or mail |
| Label old mailbox name | Helps forwarder |

Address format: [how Japanese addresses work](/residents/japan-address-system).

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Only telling your employer. **Japan Post forwarding is separate** from residence registration.

## Scams vs legitimate official letters

| Legitimate | Scam signal |
| --- | --- |
| Known utility logo | Unknown fee, gift card payment |
| Ward you registered at | Threats, instant arrest |
| Bank you use | Random URL QR |
| Registered delivery you expected | Pay fee to "release" parcel |
| Tax slip with konbini barcode | Wire transfer only |

| Scam pattern | Response |
| --- | --- |
| **Call demanding immediate transfer** | Hang up, call official number from website |
| **SMS link for "tax refund"** | Delete |
| **Fake delivery fee card** | Match tracking you ordered |

More: [phone scams and fraud](/residents/japan-phone-scams-fraud-guide).

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Panicking at kanji. **Pause, translate, verify sender.** Real offices allow time and in-person questions.

## FAQ

### Do I need a Japanese name on my mailbox?

Use the name on your lease and residence card. Consistency matters more than kanji vs romaji.

### What if I cannot read the letter?

Photograph it, translate, call the office number on the letter, or visit ward foreign desk.

### Is registered mail always bad news?

No. Banks, My Number, and cards use it routinely.

### How long before mail is returned?

Varies. Treat registered notices within **one to two days**.

### Can I pay all government mail at konbini?

Many tax and utility slips yes. Some require bank transfer only. Read the slip icons.

### What is the red stamp on envelopes?

Official seal (公印). Indicates authentic government or institution sender.

### Do I need hanko for mail?

Less than before. Signature or stamp as instructed on forms. My Number processes often use signature.

### How do I forward mail when moving?

Japan Post tenkyo service plus update [residence registration](/residents/japan-residence-registration).

### Are English inserts included?

Sometimes in Tokyo wards. Do not rely on them nationally.

### What if my name is wrong on mail?

Fix at ward office and with sender (bank, utility).

### Is email replacing paper?

Partially. Still check mailbox weekly.

### How do parcels differ from official mail?

Carriers use different slips. See [package delivery guide](/residents/japan-package-delivery-guide).
