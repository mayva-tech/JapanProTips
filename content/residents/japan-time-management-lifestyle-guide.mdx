## Why time feels different in Japan

| Factor | Daily effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Reliable transit** | You plan to the minute |
| **Dense cities** | Small delays compound |
| **Service appointments** | Slots are real boundaries |
| **Group coordination** | One late person affects many |
| **Seasonal commute shifts** | Rain, heat, pollen change timing |

Time is treated as **shared infrastructure**, not only personal preference.

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Assuming Japanese punctuality is genetic. It is mostly **systems plus social expectation** learned early.

**Scenario:** You aim for 9:00 meeting. You arrive 8:52, sign in, sit. Meeting starts 9:00 with no "waiting for Bob" energy. You adjust within a month.

## FAQ

### Is Japan always strict about punctuality?

Public transit and many workplaces yes. Friends and creative jobs vary.

### How early should I arrive for work?

Often ready at start time. Many arrive 10-15 min early first months.

### What if the train is delayed?

Companies usually accept official delay certificates (遅延証明書).

### Are restaurants strict about reservation time?

Be on time. Short grace, then call if late.

### How do daycare late pickups work?

Fees and staff stress. Treat pickup time like meeting CEO.

### Do I need a Japanese calendar app?

Any app works if alerts are reliable.

### Is being 5 minutes late to friends OK?

Often yes with message. Do not assume for work.

### How do delivery windows work?

Be available whole window or reschedule in app.

### Does remote work change punctuality?

Meetings still start on time online. Camera on time matters.
