## Why garbage rules matter in Japan

Sorted collection keeps burn lines clean, cuts landfill load, and keeps neighborhood collection points from overflowing. Mistakes can mean rejected bags, notes from building managers, or awkward talks with neighbors.

- Your address determines which chart and bags apply.
- Apartment buildings often add rules on top of city rules.
- Collection times are early morning or fixed evening windows.
- Consistent sorting is part of being a good neighbor, not a hobby.

<InfoBox title="Watch for this">
  Tourist-area habits from home rarely transfer. Treat your first month as observation: photograph the chart in your lobby and ask the manager one clarifying question.
</InfoBox>

## The most common garbage categories

Names on charts vary. Colors and kanji labels differ by city. The ideas below repeat nationwide even when labels do not match.

### Burnable trash

**Burnable** (燃やすごみ / 可燃ごみ) is kitchen waste, food-soiled paper, small wooden items, and other waste that incinerates. Think damp, organic, or greasy.

- Drain liquids from noodles and soups before you bag.
- Wrap sharp bones or shells so they do not pierce bags.
- Do not assume all paper is burnable; clean paper often has its own day.

### Non-burnable trash

**Non-burnable** (燃やさないごみ / 不燃ごみ) covers ceramics, metal tools, small appliances without batteries, and heat-resistant glass.

- Broken cups and pans usually land here, not burnable.
- Large metal may belong to oversized collection instead.
- Remove batteries from devices and handle them per local battery rules.

### Plastic packaging

**Plastic** (プラスチック / 容器包装プラ) is wrappers, trays, and film marked for plastic routes. It is not every plastic object in your home.

- Rinse light food residue off trays and cups.
- Crush air out of bags only if your ward allows it.
- Hard plastic toys and hangers often belong elsewhere; read the chart.

### PET bottles

**PET bottles** have their own route in most cities. Labels and caps often separate from the bottle body.

- Empty, rinse, and dry bottles before you set them out.
- Remove caps and labels when the chart requires it.
- Do not bag PET with plastic packaging unless your city explicitly allows combined collection.

### Cans and glass bottles

**Cans** (かん) and **glass bottles** (びん) usually share a recyclable metals and glass day, sometimes split by color.

- Rinse drink residue out of cans and bottles.
- Aluminum, steel, and glass may use separate boxes at collection points.
- Returnable shop bottles go back to shops, not the neighborhood bin.

### Paper and cardboard

**Paper** (紙) and **cardboard** (ダンボール) often use a dedicated day. Flatten boxes and tie stacks with twine when required.

- Remove tape and plastic sleeves from shipping boxes.
- Shredded paper may need a paper bag, not a plastic bag.
- Glossy flyers follow paper rules in many wards; greasy pizza boxes often do not.

### Oversized garbage

**Oversized** (粗大ごみ) covers furniture, bicycles, and large items that never fit standard bags. You usually book pickup and pay a fee.

- Call or use the ward website to schedule a slot.
- Attach the payment sticker or label your city requires.
- Do not leave oversized items at a collection point without booking.

<TipBox title="Field note">
  When in doubt, search your ward name plus the item in Japanese, or ask your building manager with a photo on your phone.
</TipBox>

## How collection days work

Most areas use a weekly rotation: burnable on Monday and Thursday, plastics on Tuesday, and so on. Your chart is the schedule.

- Set bags out only during the allowed time window.
- Bring bins back the same day when buildings require it.
- Holiday weeks shift days; watch lobby notices.
- Missed day means storage until the next slot, not a random retry.

## Why rules differ by city or ward

Japan has many independent municipalities. Each sets fees, bag vendors, and category names. Moving one train stop can change colors.

- Tokyo's 23 wards use different bag systems from each other.
- Rural towns may allow fewer categories with simpler charts.
- Suburban cities sometimes accept more combined streams than dense wards.
- Always reset your habits after a move.

## How to read your local garbage calendar

Charts arrive by mail, PDF, or lobby poster. Learn a few kanji once and reuse them all year.

| Kanji / label | Often means |
| --- | --- |
| 月火水木金土日 | Days of the week on the chart header |
| 可燃 / 燃やす | Burnable |
| 不燃 / 燃やさない | Non-burnable |
| プラ / プラスチック | Plastic packaging |
| ペットボトル | PET bottles |
| 資源 / びん・かん | Glass bottles and cans |
| 紙 / ダンボール | Paper and cardboard |
| 粗大 | Oversized pickup |

- Circle or highlight your building's collection point code if the chart lists several zones.
- Photograph both sides of folded pamphlets before you recycle the paper.

## Garbage bags and designated bags

Many cities require **official bags** sold at supermarkets and convenience stores. Wrong bags can be left behind with a sticker.

- Bag color and label match a category in your ward.
- Price per bag funds collection in many municipalities.
- Clear municipal bags differ from generic convenience-store trash bags.
- Keep one spare roll of each color so you are not caught on a busy night.

## Apartment garbage rooms and neighborhood collection points

Buildings use a **garbage room** (ゴミ置き場) or street **collection point** with cages or nets.

- Managers post hours; night disposal may be banned indoors even when the city allows early morning curb pickup.
- Tie bags tightly so crows and cats cannot open them.
- Bulky cardboard may need flat stacks in a cage, not stuffed in burnable bags.
- Shared spaces stay cleaner when everyone rinses recyclables.

## PET bottle rules

PET routes are strict because material quality matters for recycling plants.

- Remove caps and rings when required; caps often go to plastic day.
- Stomp bottles flat only if your chart shows that step.
- No motor oil or chemical bottles in PET streams.
- Small PET labels go to plastic when separated from the bottle.

## Cardboard and paper rules

Cardboard days reward flat, dry stacks.

- Break down boxes along seams; remove styrofoam and plastic air pillows.
- Tie with paper twine or place in paper bags per local photos on the chart.
- Wet or food-stained cardboard usually belongs in burnable, not paper.
- Shredded documents may need a labeled paper bag, not a plastic shopping bag.

## Oversized garbage basics

Sofas, mattresses, and AC units need **sodai gomi** booking, not creative curb placement.

- Check the ward website for item codes and fees.
- Buy the sticker at designated stores before pickup day.
- Disassemble only when the booking page says you should.
- Appliances with freon need specialist routes, not standard oversized slots.

<InfoBox>
  Leaving unbooked furniture on the street can count as illegal dumping. The fee for proper pickup is cheaper than fines and neighbor conflict.
</InfoBox>

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Using one bag for everything because it fits
- Setting bags out the night before when only early morning is allowed
- Putting pizza boxes in paper when they are greasy burnable
- Mixing PET bottles with plastic trays on the wrong day
- Ignoring building notices during Golden Week schedule changes

## Simple weekly garbage checklist

- Check tonight's category on the lobby chart or app
- Rinse recyclables and drain burnable waste
- Use the correct official bag color
- Set out during the posted time window
- Bring empty bins back and wipe the garbage room shelf if needed
- Store next categories in separate small bins inside your kitchen

## Final advice

Garbage rules reward a boring routine. One shelf in the kitchen for each stream, one photo of the chart on your phone, and one question to your manager beats guessing all year.

When you move wards, assume zero carryover. Download the new chart on day one and buy the new bags before your first full grocery run.
