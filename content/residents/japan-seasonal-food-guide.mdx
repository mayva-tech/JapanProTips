## Why seasonal food matters so much in Japan

| Reason | Daily effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Island agriculture rhythm** | Peak harvest windows short |
| **Marketing calendar** | Retail resets monthly |
| **Gift culture** | Premium fruit seasons |
| **Restaurant menus** | Limited items |
| **Home cooking tradition** | Ingredients at best once yearly |

Seasonal eating is not only fine dining. It is **supermarket endcaps and konbini shelves**.

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Thinking seasonal food is tourist-only kaiseki. Residents live it through **¥198 onigiri flavors and potato chip bags**.

**Grocery example:** October walk through Life supermarket. Sweet potato snacks, kuri chestnut desserts, Pacific saury displays. You buy one of each. That is seasonal culture at home scale.

## FAQ

### Is seasonal food expensive?

Peak items can be. In-season produce is often reasonable. Gift fruit is separate tier.

### What does 旬 (shun) mean on labels?

Best peak flavor season for that ingredient.

### Are limited edition snacks worth it?

Fun to try. Skip if budget tight.

### When are strawberries cheapest?

Often spring sales after winter premium.

### Do residents buy osechi?

Some cook, many pre-order. Konbini also sells sets.

### How do I find seasonal vegetables?

Supermarket produce section endcaps and flyers.

### Is autumn really saury season?

Sanma popular autumn, cheap at markets.

### What is summer unagi day?

Marketing tradition around Doyo no Ushi. Eel promotions spike.

### Can I eat seasonally on a budget?

Yes. Mikan, sweet potato, cabbage cycles, discount stickers.
