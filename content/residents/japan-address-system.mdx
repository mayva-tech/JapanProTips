## Why Japanese addresses confuse foreigners

Visitors expect a street name and house number. Japan often uses area blocks, building names, and room numbers instead. Maps still work because pins use coordinates, but paperwork looks unfamiliar at first.

- The same building can look different in romaji and Japanese order.
- Apartment brands repeat across cities, so names alone are not unique.
- Postal codes cover areas, not single doors, yet mail still arrives with a full address.
- A missing room number is the most common delivery failure.

<InfoBox title="Watch for this">
  Copy addresses from your lease or management company PDF. Do not guess block numbers from a map screenshot.
</InfoBox>

## The basic structure of a Japanese address

Read top to bottom on official forms. Each line narrows the location.

1. **Postal code** (〒123-4567)
2. **Prefecture** (都道府県), for example Tokyo or Osaka
3. **City or ward** (市区町村), for example Shibuya-ku
4. **District or town area** (町名), often ending in chome
5. **Block and lot numbers** (番地), ban and go
6. **Building name and room** (建物名・部屋番号)

  Save one screenshot of your full Japanese address in Notes. Paste it into forms instead of retyping from memory.

## Prefecture, city, ward, district, block, building

**Prefecture** is the largest unit, like a state. **Tokyo** is both a prefecture and a metro area with special wards inside.

**City** (市) covers many municipalities. **Ward** (区) appears inside designated cities such as Tokyo's 23 wards.

**District** names describe neighborhoods. You will see them on maps and station exits before you see a street label.

**Block** refers to numbered zones within a chome. **Building** is the tower, house, or shop front that carriers actually seek.

## Understanding chome, ban, and go numbers

**Chome** (丁目) divides a district into numbered sections, written like 1-chome or 一丁目.

**Ban** (番) marks a block within the chome. **Go** (号) is the lot or building slot within that block.

Example pattern (not a real address):

`2-chome, 14 ban, 7 go` → area section 2, block 14, lot 7

- Hyphens and commas differ by form; follow your lease spelling.
- Zero-padding varies; use the exact digits on your contract.
- Rural areas may use simpler lot numbers with fewer chome layers.

## Apartment and mansion naming systems

**Mansion** (マンション) usually means a reinforced concrete apartment building, not a luxury villa. **Apato** (アパート) often means wood or light steel smaller buildings.

- Building names appear after block numbers: `Shibuya Heights 403`.
- Room format is often floor plus unit: `403` on the 4th floor, unit 03.
- Management companies add English names on foreign-facing leases.
- Deliveries need building name, room number, and buzzer name if required.

## Postal codes in Japan

Postal codes use seven digits: `123-4567`. The first three digits narrow the area; the last four narrow delivery zones.

- Look up codes on Japan Post tools when you only have a map pin.
- One code can cover many buildings; the rest of the address still matters.
- Write 〒 before the code on envelopes when the form shows that symbol.

## Why streets often have no names

Many neighborhoods grew in grids labeled by blocks, not named roads. Major avenues have names, but residential lanes may be unlabeled.

- Directions use landmarks: station exits, konbini corners, rivers.
- Police boxes (koban) and post offices anchor local directions.
- Do not insist on street names when talking to older neighbors; use block numbers.

## How navigation apps handle Japanese addresses

Google Maps, Apple Maps, and Japan-specific apps accept pasted Japanese addresses well.

- Search in Japanese for best pin placement, then switch language for reading.
- Drop the pin on the building entrance, not the station you used to walk there.
- Save **Home** after you confirm the pin at your door once at night and once by day.
- Taxi apps let you paste Japanese; show the screen to the driver if audio fails.

| App habit | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Paste full address from lease | Avoids wrong chome |
| Add room number in memo field | Drivers stop at the correct entrance |
| Screenshot entrance photo | Helps couriers in dense blocks |

## Writing your address correctly in Japan

Official forms want consistent order and exact kanji from your lease.

- Match prefecture, city, ward, and chome spelling character for character.
- Include building and room on the same line if the form is short.
- Use your name in katakana or kanji as registered on your residence card when asked.
- Double-check postal code against Japan Post after any move.

## Romanized vs Japanese-format addresses

Romaji helps you read aloud. Japanese characters are what clerks and systems expect.

- Residence registration and bank forms usually need Japanese.
- International mail may ask for country line in English and local lines in Japanese.
- Do not invent macrons or alternate spellings once your lease fixes the romaji.
- Keep one Japanese block and one romaji block in your phone for copy paste.

## Address registration and official documents

Your **registered address** lives at the ward office and on your residence card after you complete moving-in procedures.

- Lease address and registered address should match unless staff approve exceptions.
- Health insurance and pension mail route to the registered record.
- Update within the required window when you move; see the residence registration guide.
- **Juminhyo** (resident record copies) show the official address for bank KYC.

<InfoBox>
  If you live in company housing, HR or the dorm manager may handle registration steps. Ask who holds the master lease before you visit city hall alone.
</InfoBox>

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Swapping ban and go numbers on forms
- Omitting the building name when the block number points to land, not a tower
- Using only a station name for food delivery
- Registering a short-stay hotel as a permanent address
- Translating chome as "street" on international sites that reject the form

## Simple address-reading checklist

- Find 〒 postal code on your lease
- Identify prefecture and city or ward
- Locate chome, then ban, then go
- Add building name in Japanese as printed
- Add room number and your name on the buzzer if required
- Test one delivery or map pin before you rely on it for emergencies

## Final advice

Addresses are a system, not a trivia quiz. Copy faithfully, confirm once with a map pin at your door, and keep the same text everywhere official systems ask.

When you move, update apps, banks, and city hall in the same week. Old addresses ghost you through bills and missed packages longer than you expect.
