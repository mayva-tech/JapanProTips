## Why city halls intimidate foreign residents

Common stress points:

- **Japanese signage** and ticket machines
- Fear of doing something **illegal by accident**
- Long forms with kanji fields
- **Quiet halls** where everyone seems to know the script
- Multiple desks for insurance, tax, and registration

The staff see newcomers every week. You are not the first foreign resident to ask for help finding the right line.

## City hall vs ward office explained

| Term | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Shiyakusho** (city hall) | Main office of a **city** government |
| **Kuyakusho** (ward office) | Branch office for a **ward** inside a big city (Tokyo has 23) |
| **Machizukuri center** | Smaller local service point in some towns |
| **Branch office** | Sub-office for distant neighborhoods |

**Tokyo example:** You live in Shibuya-ku. You go to **Shibuya Ward Office** (*Shibuya-kuyakusho*), not Tokyo Metropolitan Government, for residence registration.

**Smaller city example:** You live in Sapporo. You may use **Sapporo City Hall** or a ward branch depending on address.

Rule: use the office that **covers your registered address**.

## Residence registration process

**Jumin toroku** (resident registration) ties you to a municipality.

Typical first visit:

1. Take a **ticket** for residence registration.
2. Submit form, residence card, lease proof.
3. Receive stamped copies and next-step instructions.
4. Enroll **NHI** and **pension** if staff route you in same trip.

Full walkthrough: [residence registration guide](/residents/japan-residence-registration).

| Document (common) | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Residence card | ID and status |
| Passport | Backup ID |
| Lease or housing proof | Address verification |
| Completed moving-in form | Municipality version |

**Timeline:** Same day for registration processing at counter. Insurance cards may arrive by mail later.

## Address change procedures

When you move **within Japan**:

1. **Moving-out** (*tenshutsu*) at old ward office (or notify both in some flows).
2. **Moving-in** (*tennyu*) at new ward office within deadline.
3. Update **bank, employer, phone**, and My Number card address.

<InfoBox title="Foreign residents panic about this">
  Skipping moving-out because you are "still in Tokyo" causes mail and tax records to split wrong. Two wards, two sets of bills, one confused you.
</InfoBox>

**Example:** Shinjuku to Meguro on March 28. Submit moving-out at Shinjuku, moving-in at Meguro by deadline. Bring new lease and residence card both days if possible.

## National health insurance enrollment

**Kokuho** (National Health Insurance) is handled at the municipal desk, often right after registration.

| Step | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Submit enrollment form | Staff link to your address record |
| Receive insurance card timing | May be on the spot or by mail |
| Premium notices | Mailed later based on income |

Guide: [National Health Insurance for residents](/residents/japan-health-insurance).

## Pension enrollment

Pension desk or Japan Pension Service directions after registration.

| Track | Who |
| --- | --- |
| **Employees' pension** | Employer usually handles |
| **National pension** | Students, freelancers, part-time without coverage |

You may receive enrollment papers at the counter or by mail. Keep them in your pension folder.

Guide: [Japan pension system](/residents/japan-pension-system).

## My Number card procedures

At the ward office you may:

- Receive **My Number notification**
- Apply for **Individual Number Card**
- **Pick up** the card with PIN setup
- Update **address** on municipal record (card surface update may be separate)

Guide: [My Number Card for foreign residents](/residents/japan-my-number-card).

## Certificates and official documents

Common requests at the counter or konbini with My Number Card:

| Certificate | Japanese name | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Residence certificate** | *Juminhyo* | Landlord, bank, school |
| **Residence certificate with ID** | *Juminhyo shohon* | Formal proof |
| **Tax certificate** | *Kazei shomei* | Visa, contracts |
| **Seal registration** | *Inkan toroku* | Less common for new foreigners |

| Method | Fee | Speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter same day | Small fee per copy | Minutes to short wait |
| Konbini with card | Fee + machine | After hours |

Ask for **English** whether they can issue bilingual forms. Many certificates are Japanese only.

## How ticket systems and counters work

Most offices use a **numbered ticket** (*seiriken*) system.

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Choose procedure on touchscreen (icons + Japanese) |
| 2 | Take printed ticket with **number** |
| 3 | Sit in waiting area until screen or voice calls number |
| 4 | Go to indicated **counter window** |
| 5 | Hand papers, answer questions, receive stamps |

<TipBox title="Survival tip">
  Photograph your ticket number. If you miss your turn, you may need a new ticket and wait again.
</TipBox>

Peak times: **Monday morning**, day after holidays, **lunch hour** before closing.

| Reality | What helps |

| **Foreign resident section** in big wards | Shibuya, Shinjuku, Minato, etc. |
| Simple phrase list | See below |

| English | Japanese (polite) |
| --- | --- |
| Moving in registration | Tennyu todoke onegaishimasu |
| Moving out | Tenshutsu todoke onegaishimasu |
| National health insurance | Kokumin kenko hoken |
| Residence certificate | Juminhyo |

Staff appreciate calm tone and **pre-filled forms** more than perfect Japanese.

## Waiting time expectations

| Visit type | Wait mindset |
| --- | --- |
| Certificate only, off-peak | 10-30 minutes |
| Registration + insurance, peak | 45-90+ minutes |

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Going to **wrong municipality** (friend's ward, company HQ ward)
- **Hotel address** as permanent registration
- Missing **moving-out** when leaving a ward
- Losing **stamped copies** with no photos
- Wrong **ticket button** and long wrong queue
- Assuming **one visit** covers employer pension (employer may still need forms)
- Ignoring **Japanese mail** from city hall

## What Japanese staff actually expect

| Expectation | Your move |
| --- | --- |
| Correct forms for procedure | Download PDF beforehand |
| Original documents | Not only phone photos |
| Calm, sequential answers | Do not rush at window |
| Listen for **next desk** directions | Insurance after registration |
| Take **all stamped copies** offered | Do not refuse copies |
| Thank you (*arigato gozaimashita*) | Basic courtesy |

They do not expect flawless Japanese. They expect **correct procedure and valid documents**.

## FAQ

### Is city hall the same as immigration?

No. Immigration is national. City hall is **municipal** residence and local services.

### Do I need an appointment?

Depends on ward. Walk-in works many places. Appointments reduce wait in Tokyo wards that offer them.

### Can I register at a hotel?

Usually not as a long-term address. Use legal residence address.

### How soon after moving must I register?

Deadlines apply (commonly within 14 days of move). Confirm current rule on official site.

### Do both spouses visit?

One person may submit if documents allow. Married couples sometimes register together for clarity.

### Will they keep my residence card?

They inspect and return it unless a special case applies.

### Can I get certificates for my employer same day?

Often yes for *juminhyo* at counter.

### What if I cannot speak Japanese?

Bring a friend, use ward foreign desk, or write key phrases on paper.

### Does moving require a new My Number?

Number stays. Address record updates. Card may need update application.

### Where do I pay resident tax?

Often separate envelopes by mail or employer collection. Tax desk at city hall answers questions.
