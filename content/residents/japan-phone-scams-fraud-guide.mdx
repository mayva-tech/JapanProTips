## Why scams in Japan feel different from other countries

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **High trust in institutions** | Fake "official" messages sound believable |
| **Japanese language pressure** | Urgency plus kanji pushes quick clicks |
| **Cash and transfer culture** | Bank transfers used for real bills |
| **Low street crime** | Complacency online |
| **New resident stress** | Visa and housing deadlines |

Scammers exploit **procedure**, not chaos. They mimic delivery slips, bank alerts, and immigration tone.

## The reality: Japan is safe, but scams still exist

| True | Also true |
| --- | --- |
| Violent street crime is low | Financial fraud happens |
| Most neighbors are honest | A few targeted campaigns exist |
| Banks have fraud teams | You must report quickly |
| Police take cyber cases | Recovery is not guaranteed |

| Your goal | Not your goal |
| --- | --- |
| Spot patterns early | Never answer unknown numbers |
| Verify before paying | Stop using all apps |
| Report once | Obsess over every SMS |

Awareness beats anxiety. One checklist handles most situations.

## Fake immigration and government calls

Immigration and police **do not demand immediate wire transfers** by phone.

| Fake claim | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "Pay fine or deport tomorrow" | Real notices are written, with process |
| "Your visa is cancelled" | Check status online or at office |
| "Do not contact your employer" | Isolation tactic |

| Verify path | Contact |
| --- | --- |
| Immigration status | Official Immigration Services Agency website |
| Ward tax or city | Ward office number from official site |
| Police matter | Visit koban or call 110 if crime in progress |

Visa help: [renewing your visa](/residents/japan-renewing-visa-guide). City hall: [city hall guide](/residents/japan-city-hall-guide).

<InfoBox title="Foreign residents panic about this">
  **One scary call does not cancel your visa.** Stop, hang up, and confirm through official channels before sending money or personal documents.
</InfoBox>

## Delivery SMS scams and phishing links

Fake **Yamato, Sagawa, or Japan Post** texts are common.

| Real SMS pattern | Fake SMS pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Short, specific tracking link to carrier domain | Random URL shorteners |
| You ordered something recently | "Package held, pay fee" when you did not shop |
| Japanese with your name sometimes | Odd grammar, urgent fee |

| Example fake text (illustrative) | Problem |
| --- | --- |
| "Delivery failed. Pay ¥98 here: bit.ly/xxxxx" | Fee + wrong domain |
| "Confirm address within 2 hours" | Pressure + unknown link |

| Check | How |
| --- | --- |
| Open shopping app order page | Not SMS link |
| Compare tracking number | Must match order |
| Type carrier URL manually | Avoid tap |

## FAQ

### Is Japan dangerous for scams?

Overall crime is low. Financial scams exist but are manageable with verification habits.

### Will police help in English?

Major stations often have some support. Bring a friend or use translation for reports.

### Should I answer unknown Japanese numbers?

Let voicemail collect. Legit callers leave messages or send official mail.

### Are delivery SMS always fake?

No. Compare with orders and check domains before tapping links.

### Can I get money back after transfer?

Sometimes if reported within minutes. Not guaranteed. Act fast.

### How do I spot rental scams?

No viewing, personal account transfer, price far below market, stock photos.

### Is PayPay safe?

Yes with normal habits. Do not send to strangers or scan unknown QR codes.

### What is #9110 in Japan?

Police consultation line for non-emergency advice.

### Should I post my residence card online?

Avoid. Cover number if required for admin only.

### Are credit cards safer than bank transfer?

For purchases, chargeback options exist. Transfers are final faster.

### Do scammers target English speakers?

Yes. Bilingual messages happen. Same verification rules apply.

### Where do I report phishing sites?

Police plus your email provider. Bank if you entered credentials.
