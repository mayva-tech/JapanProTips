## Why vending machines are everywhere in Japan

Vending machines (**jidohanbaiki**) stay profitable here because people actually use them, operators restock often, and crime rates on street units stay low enough that machines can live outside, unattended, for years.

- **Dense cities:** A machine on a quiet block still gets foot traffic from commuters and students.
- **Cash and digital habits:** Coins, bills, and IC taps are normal. Machines were upgraded early for contactless.
- **Trust in food handling:** Sealed drinks and factory-packed snacks reduce the "street food risk" feeling.
- **24-hour rhythm:** Trains, konbini, and vending overlap so you are never far from hydration.
- **Low vandalism:** Broken glass and theft happen, but not at the scale that kills outdoor retail in many countries.

They are infrastructure, not a novelty exhibit. Tourists notice them because there are **so many**, not because each one is unique.

## What tourists are surprised by

<InfoBox title="Tourist surprise">
  - **Hot drinks in summer.** Red labels under buttons mean warm cans, not "sold out."
  - **Machines that sell hot meals** next to machines that sell mascara or bananas (in some neighborhoods).
  - **Perfect change every time** when you pay cash. The machine is a cashier with no small talk.
  - **IC card works** on many units but not all. One dead tap does not mean your card is broken.
  - **Almost no trash can** beside the machine. You carry the empty can until a konbini or station bin.
</InfoBox>

<TipBox title="Quick tip">
  Stand to the side while you decide. Locals choose fast. Block the sidewalk only if the machine is set back from the path.
</TipBox>

## What you can buy from Japanese vending machines

Most machines are drink-first. Specialty machines add food and oddities.

### Drinks

- Water, sparkling water, sports drinks
- Green tea, barley tea (**mugicha**), oolong
- Cola, cider-style sodas, energy drinks
- Juice boxes and kid-size cartons

### Coffee

- Black hot coffee, café au lait, latte cans
- Cold brew and milk coffee in summer
- Brand rows (Boss, Georgia, Wonda) that repeat on every block

### Ice cream

- Frozen bars and cups behind a separate freezer door
- Soft-serve style cups at some highway stops
- Melts fast. Eat within a minute or bag it for a konbini freezer later (not ideal).

### Ramen and hot meals

- **Cup ramen** drops from some machines (bring hot water or buy elsewhere too)
- **Hot frozen meals** (curry rice, pasta) heated inside the machine in premium units
- Not a dinner strategy every night. Fun once, then use [restaurants](/guides/japan-restaurant-guide) or konbini.

### Umbrellas

- Cheap vinyl umbrellas when rain starts suddenly
- Better than nothing when you are two stops from a store

### Batteries

- AA and AAA packs at electronics-heavy areas and some hotels
- Prices are convenience premium. Fine in a pinch.

### Hot meals (heated in machine)

- Full trays behind glass on **hot food** machines near offices and stations
- Quality is "office lunch reliable," not restaurant level

## Hot vs cold drinks explained

Buttons show **red** (warm) and **blue** (cold) bands on many drink machines. Read the strip above the row, not only the bottle photo.

| Label color | Temperature | Common examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Hot | Coffee, black tea, corn soup cans |
| Blue | Cold | Water, cola, iced tea |

<TipBox title="Quick tip">
  Afternoon in July? Double-check you did not buy hot corn soup by mistake. Tourist photos love the mistake. Your hands will not.
</TipBox>

Some machines mix both sides in one panel. Slow down one second before you press.

## How to pay

### Coins

- Insert **coins first** on older machines before bills.
- ¥10, ¥50, ¥100, ¥500 work on most units. ¥1 and ¥5 may not count toward price.
- Machine shows remaining balance on a small display after you insert money.

### Cash (bills)

- Feed **¥1000** bills on modern machines. Some take ¥2000 and ¥5000.
- Change drops as coins below the tray.
- Keep a **¥1000 coin or two** in your pocket for machines that reject worn foreign bills (rare but possible).

### Suica and PASMO

- Look for **transportation card** icons (Suica, PASMO, IC).
- Tap once on the reader. Wait for **OK** before you grab the drink.
- If balance is low, top up at a station machine or on your phone.

### IC cards (other)

- Some machines show **QuicPay**, **iD**, or **WAON** marks for Japanese bank cards and phone wallets.
- Tourist debit cards may not work on every contactless rail.

### QR payments

- PayPay and other QR logos appear on newer machines.
- Scan with the app, confirm amount, wait for dispense.
- Setup requires a Japanese or supported wallet. Many short-term visitors skip this and use cash or Suica.

| Payment | Works often? | Tourist tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ¥100 / ¥500 coins | Yes | Fastest when you have change |
| ¥1000 bill | Yes | Use when coin slot is full |
| Suica / PASMO | Often | Load ¥2000+ for drinks and metro |
| Credit card tap | Sometimes | Look for contactless logo |
| Foreign Apple Pay | Hit or miss | Carry cash backup |

## Where vending machines are usually found

- Train stations (platforms and exits)
- Residential side streets (every 100-200 meters in cities)
- Highway rest areas (**PA/SA**)
- Office districts and university corners
- Hotels and tourist temples (fewer in deep rural villages)
- Parks and tourist viewpoints (sometimes overpriced, still handy)

If you see three machines in a row, they are often **different companies** (Coca-Cola, DyDo, Kirin), not three copies of the same stock.

## Convenience stores vs vending machines

| | Vending machine | Konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours | 24/7 on the street | 24/7 store |
| Selection | Drinks plus limited snacks | Full meals, ATMs, tickets, toilets |
| Price | Slightly higher on drinks | More choices, similar drink prices |
| Trash | Rare nearby | Bins inside |
| Speed | 30 seconds | 3-5 minutes inside |

Use machines for **hydration between stops**. Use konbini when you need food variety, Wi-Fi, or a bathroom. Read [Japanese convenience stores explained](/guides/japan-convenience-store-guide) for the full konbini playbook.

## Why Japanese vending machines are trusted

- Products arrive **sealed** from factories. You are not buying from an open cooler.
- Operators **rotate stock** and clean glass on busy routes.
- **Price is printed** on the button. No haggling, no surprise fees.
- **Refund culture** for stuck products: call the free number on the machine with the location code if your item hangs (see safety section).

Tourist trust should still be **visual**: dented cans, expired dates (rare), or broken glass mean walk to the next machine.

## Pricing and realistic expectations

Drink prices in cities often land around **¥110-¥180** for standard cans and bottles in 2026, with premium coffee or large bottles higher.

- Temple areas and airports can run **¥20-¥50** above neighborhood machines.
- Hot meal machines can hit **¥400-¥800** for a tray.
- Umbrellas: **¥500-¥700** quick buys.

Budget your trip with real ranges in [Japan trip cost breakdown](/guides/japan-budget-breakdown). Vending is small money daily, but two drinks a day adds up over two weeks.

## Weird vending machines tourists search for

Social media highlights oddballs. You might see some, you might not.

- **Banana** and fresh fruit machines (limited locations)
- **Burgers** and frozen food lockers
- **SIM cards** and electronics in airport zones
- **Rice** and regional souvenir machines in tourism towns
- **Mystery toy** capsule machines (gachapon, different mechanism but same sidewalk energy)

</InfoBox>

## Etiquette and trash disposal

- Do not open drinks beside the machine and leave spills.
- **Carry empties** until a konbini trash zone or station bin.
- Recycling rules vary by city. Cans often go in **can-only** slots when available.
- Line up if others are waiting. One person selects at a time.
- Help short visitors reach buttons if you can; it is normal kindness, not required.

Japan has few public bins by design. Pair this guide with [toilets, trash, and public etiquette](/guides/how-to-use-japanese-toilets) so vending trash does not surprise you on day one.

## Common tourist mistakes

- Tapping IC once and walking away before the **OK** light
- Buying **hot** drinks when they wanted cold
- Shaking the machine when an item sticks (use the refund number instead)
- Blocking the sidewalk during a long photo session
- Assuming **credit card** works because Apple Pay works at Uniqlo
- Throwing **recycling** in the wrong konbini bin
- Treating vending dinner as cheaper than konbini (often it is not)

<TipBox title="Beginner reassurance">
  Every tourist buys one wrong-temperature drink once. You are still doing fine.
</TipBox>

## Safety and scams

Vending machines in Japan are generally **safe**. Serious scams are rare compared with tourist areas in other countries.

- **Stuck product:** Note the machine ID, call the toll-free number on the sticker, follow prompts. Many refund same day or send a coupon code.
- **Fake machines:** Extremely rare. Use machines with brand logos and power lights.
- **Overly friendly "helpers":** If someone insists they fix the machine for cash, use the official refund line instead.
- **Drink tampering:** Sealed cans and bottles are standard. Skip anything with a broken seal.

If a machine eats your money with no product, do not punch the glass. Staff at nearby shops often know the refill company phone.

## FAQ

### Do Japan vending machines take credit cards?

Some do. **Suica, PASMO, coins, and ¥1000 bills** are the reliable tourist trio. Carry cash backup.

### Can tourists use Suica on vending machines?

Yes on many machines with IC icons. Top up first. Low balance fails quietly.

### Are vending machines cheaper than konbini?

Drinks are **close in price**. Konbini wins for food variety and bathrooms.

### Why are drinks hot and cold in the same machine?

Dual-zone heating and cooling. Color labels show which row is which.

### Is vending machine water safe to drink?

Sealed bottles from major brands are standard. Check seal and expiry like anywhere.

### What if my drink gets stuck?

Use the **refund hotline** on the machine label with the location code. Do not rock the machine hard.

### Do machines give change for ¥1000?

Modern machines do. Older units may only take coins.

### Are street vending machines open at night?

Yes. That is half the point.

### Can I recycle the can at the machine?

Rarely. Take it to konbini or station sorting bins.

### Are gachapon the same as drink machines?

Different mechanism (capsule turn knob) but often lined up together on tourist streets.
