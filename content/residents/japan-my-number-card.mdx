## What the My Number Card actually is

The **Individual Number Card** (個人番号カード, often called **My Number Card**) is a plastic card with your photo that links to your **12-digit My Number** (マイナンバー).

| Feature | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Photo ID** | Official identification in many situations |
| **IC chip** | Digital certificates and some online services |
| **Not a residence card** | Immigration status stays on your residence card |
| **Optional to apply** | The number exists before the card |

The card is optional, but many residents choose it for convenience.

## My Number number vs My Number Card

| | **My Number (12 digits)** | **My Number Card** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **When you get it** | After residence registration | When you apply and receive mail |
| **Looks like** | Paper notification letter | Plastic photo card |
| **Required?** | Assigned by system | Application is voluntary |
| **Main use** | Tax, insurance, pension linkage | ID + digital services + konbini certificates |

<InfoBox title="Foreign residents get confused by this">
  Receiving a **My Number notification** in the mail does not mean you already have the card. The card is a separate application with photo and PIN setup.
</InfoBox>

## Who can get one

Generally:

- **Mid- to long-term residents** with a valid residence card
- People registered at a municipal address in Japan
- Adults and minors (minors need guardian steps)

Short-term tourists cannot apply. Status and municipality rules apply. Check your ward office page for edge cases (new arrivals, pending address registration).

## Why residents eventually need it

You can live without the card for a while. Many residents apply when:

- Employers ask for My Number for payroll
- **Tax filing** and municipal tax forms need the number
- **Health insurance** enrollment links to your record
- You want **konbini certificates** without visiting the ward office
- **Banking** or phone contracts ask for identity documents
- You use **online government services** that prefer the card

The **number** matters for bureaucracy. The **card** matters for convenience.

## Common uses

### Government procedures

- Identity verification at counters when accepted
- Some online portals with card reader or smartphone linkage
- Faster identity checks when rules allow card instead of residence card plus extra papers

### Health insurance

- Enrollment uses your My Number behind the scenes
- Card may simplify proof when updating insurance at the ward office
- Not a health insurance card replacement (keep your insurer card)

See [National Health Insurance for residents](/residents/japan-health-insurance).

### Tax filing

- Employers and tax offices use My Number for income reporting
- Year-end adjustment and residence tax linkage
- Card helps with some digital tax steps; number is the core requirement

Future deep dive: [taxes guide for residents](/residents/japan-taxes-guide).

### Convenience store certificates

Major chains let you print **residence certificate** (*juminhyo*) and other documents using the card IC chip at the multifunction machine.

- Saves a weekday trip to city hall
- Service hours and fees apply
- English UI limited; staff help at store sometimes

### Banking

- Some banks accept My Number Card as ID when opening accounts
- My Number may be requested separately for tax reporting
- Policies vary by bank branch

See [open a bank account in Japan](/residents/open-bank-account-japan).

| Use | Needs number only | Card helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payroll with employer | Yes | Sometimes |
| NHI enrollment | Yes | Sometimes |
| Konbini certificates | No | Yes |
| Photo ID at counter | No | Yes |
| Digital government login | Partial | Often yes |

## How to apply step-by-step

Typical first application flow (municipality may vary):

1. **Complete residence registration** at your ward or city office.
2. Receive **My Number notification** by mail (or at registration).
3. Apply for the card via **online application** (mail-in) or **in-person** at municipal office.
4. Submit **photo** meeting official specs (size, background, recent).
5. Receive **application acceptance** notice.
6. Wait for **issuance notice** (pickup or mail delivery options).
7. Pick up card in person, set **PIN codes** at pickup counter or designated machine.

<TipBox title="Government office tip">
  Book an appointment online if your municipality offers it. Walk-in queues spike after long holidays.
</TipBox>

**Example:** You register in Setagaya on Tuesday. You apply online for the card the same week with a konbini-taken photo file. Issuance notice arrives in 3-5 weeks. You pick up at the ward office Friday evening with residence card and application notice.

## How long the process takes

| Stage | Typical timeline |
| --- | --- |
| Notification letter after registration | Days to weeks |
| Application processing | About **1-4 weeks** common |
| Mail delivery option (if offered) | Extra days |
| Reissue after loss | Longer |

Peak seasons (April moves, January) run slower. Plan ahead if an employer deadline looms.

## Residence card relationship explained

| Document | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Residence card** | Immigration status, re-entry, part-time work permission |
| **My Number notification** | Shows your 12-digit number |
| **My Number Card** | Domestic ID and digital services |

Employers and banks may ask for **both** residence card and My Number information. They serve different legal roles.

## Digital features and PIN codes

The card chip supports:

- **User certificate** for online government services
- **Konbini certificate printing**
- **Some municipal apps** with card reader

You usually set:

| PIN | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **4-digit PIN** | General unlock |
| **6-16 digit signature password** | Digital signatures online |

<InfoBox title="Foreign residents get confused by this">
  Forgetting PINs is common. Reset procedures exist but take time. Store PINs in a password manager, not a group chat screenshot.
</InfoBox>

## Updating address after moving

When you **move and register a new address**:

1. Complete **moving-in registration** at new ward office.
2. My Number **system record** updates with address.
3. **Card surface address** may need update via card update application.
4. Follow municipal instructions for **sticker** or reissue.

Do not assume the chip updates every display automatically without steps.

See [moving out guide](/residents/japan-moving-out-guide) for leave-Japan flows.

## Expiration and renewal rules

| Topic | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Card expiry** | Printed on card; renewal before expiry |
| **Adult renewals** | New photo often required |
| **Child to adult** | Transition rules apply |
| **Lost card** | Report and reissue promptly |

Set a calendar reminder **2 months before expiry**.

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Confusing **notification letter** with finished card
- Missing **pickup deadline** (card returned to issuer)
- Weak or non-compliant **photo** causing rejection
- Sharing **PIN** or leaving card at workplace overnight
- Not updating **address** on card after move
- Giving My Number to **random websites** without reason
- Laminating or punching holes in the card (damages chip)

## Privacy concerns and misunderstandings

| Myth | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "Everyone can look up my number" | Access is restricted to authorized institutions |
| "The card tracks my location" | Not a GPS device |
| "I must give it to any shop" | Only when legally required or contracted |
| "Employer can keep my card" | They verify; you retain the card |

You may ask **why** an organization needs the number. Legitimate payroll and tax uses are normal.

## FAQ

### Do I automatically receive the card when I register?

No. You receive the **number notification**. The card requires application.

### Can I work without the card?

Often yes if you provide My Number to employer by notification paper. Card is not always mandatory for employment.

### Is the card the same as a residence card?

No. Residence card is immigration. My Number Card is municipal ID linked to tax and social systems.

### Can short-term students apply?

Mid- to long-term residents with proper registration generally can. Confirm with your school and ward office.

### What if I refuse the card?

You still have a My Number. Some services are harder without the card.

### Can I apply in English?

Application screens vary. Bring a Japanese-speaking friend or use municipal foreign resident desk if available.

### How much does it cost?

First issuance is often **free** or low fee by municipality. Reissue after loss usually has a fee.

### Does the card replace health insurance card?

No. Keep your insurer card for clinics.

### Can I use it at airport immigration?

No. Use your **residence card** for immigration officers.

### What if pickup notice expires?

Contact the issuing office quickly. Reapplication may be required.
