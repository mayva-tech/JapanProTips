## Why the Japanese school system feels overwhelming to foreign families

| Stress point | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **April school year** | Different from many countries' August/September |
| **Japanese-first classrooms** | English support varies |
| **Zoning** | Address decides default public school |
| **Uniforms and supply lists** | Long exact lists in Japanese |
| **PTA expectations** | Volunteer culture strong |
| **Low absence culture** | Attendance norms strict |

You are not expected to understand everything on day one. Schools and wards have dealt with foreign families before.

## Public vs private vs international schools

| Type | Tuition | Language | Who it fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Municipal public** | Free tuition (fees for lunch, trips) | Japanese primary | Long-term residents, immersion goal |
| **Private (Japanese)** | Paid | Japanese | Specific curriculum or religion |
| **International school** | High tuition | English or bilingual | Short stay, repatriation plan, language priority |

| Compare | Public | International |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Low direct tuition | ¥1.5M-¥3M+ per year common |
| Peers | Local neighborhood | Global mix |
| Commute | Often walkable | Bus or train across city |
| University path | Japanese system | IB/AP to overseas colleges |

Many families use **public for immersion** or **international for continuity**. Both are valid.

## School year and grade structure explained

Japan school year runs **April to March**.

| Grade | Typical age (April start) |
| --- | --- |
| **Yochien** (kindergarten, not compulsory) | 3-5 |
| **Shogakko** (elementary) grades 1-6 | 6-11 |
| **Chugakko** (junior high) grades 1-3 | 12-14 |
| **Koko** (high school) grades 1-3 | 15-17 |

**Example:** Child born July 2018 enters **elementary grade 1** in April 2024 with classmates who turn 6 that school year (cutoff rules use April 2 framework historically; confirm current municipal age calculation).

Long breaks: **summer** (late July-August), **winter** (year end), **spring** (March-April).

## Compulsory education in Japan

**Compulsory education** covers **elementary and junior high** (grades 1-9 equivalent).

| Stage | Compulsory? |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | No (very common socially) |
| Elementary | Yes |
| Junior high | Yes |
| High school | No (most still attend) |

Foreign national children living in Japan generally fall under the same rules when registered as residents. Confirm with ward if visa status is short-term tourist (different).

## Enrollment procedures for foreign residents

Typical **public school enrollment** flow:

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Complete **residence registration** at ward office |
| 2 | Contact **ward education department** or assigned school |
| 3 | Submit enrollment forms, residence card copies, vaccination records |
| 4 | School visit or orientation |
| 5 | Start date coordinated (often April or mid-year if moving) |

Mid-year arrivals happen. Schools place by grade and language support availability.

## School zoning and address registration

**School district** (*school ku*) follows registered address.

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Move home | May change assigned school |
| Rent in zone A | Child usually attends zone A public school |
| Request different school | Exception process, not guaranteed |

Register address before assuming a school visit. Realtors sometimes list school name for families.

Address system: linked from residence registration at ward office.

## Support systems for foreign students

| Program | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Ward foreign student desk** | Guidance in English sometimes |
| **JSL pull-out classes** | Extra Japanese lessons |
| **Adaptation guidance** | Counselors |
| **Intercultural coordinators** | Larger cities |

Tokyo wards like Shibuya, Setagaya, and Minato have stronger foreign-family resources than rural towns. Ask explicitly what your ward offers.

## School lunches and kyushoku culture

**Kyushoku** (給食) is school lunch, often cooked at school or central kitchen.

| Fact | Parent note |
| --- | --- |
| **Monthly menu** | Sent home in Japanese |
| **Allergy forms** | Submit early (*allergy* and *anaphylaxis* need clear docs) |
| **Milk and bread norms** | Standard menus |
| **Payment** | Monthly fee |
| **Students serve food** | Rotation duties |

Some schools allow **bento** on limited days; policy varies.

## Uniforms, supplies, and hidden costs

| Cost | Public school mindset |
| --- | --- |
| **Uniform** (junior high often) | ¥10,000-¥40,000+ |
| **Gym uniform** | Required set |
| **Randoseru backpack** (grade 1 tradition) | Major purchase year |
| **Exact supply list** | Pens, tissues, gym shoes indoor |
| **Excursions** | Several per year |
| **PTA fee** | Annual |
| **After-school club** | Activity fees |

Budget **¥50,000-¥150,000** setup in first year beyond lunch fees, depending on grade and school.

## Parent involvement expectations

| Expectation | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| **Read communication notebooks** (*renrakucho*) | Daily |
| **Attend conferences** (*sankan*) | Scheduled days |
| **Volunteer days** | Few per year |
| **Sports day, culture festival** | Annual big events |

Full-time working parents participate differently. Communicate constraints early. Schools often accept partial roles.

## PTA culture explained realistically

**PTA** (*kyoiku iinkai* parent group) runs events and fundraising.

| Reality | Tip |
| --- | --- |
| Strong social network | Good for local integration |
| Time consuming for officers | Decline officer roles if needed |
| Japanese meetings | Bring translator friend first year |
| Not legally mandatory | Social pressure exists but you choose level |

Declining every request isolates you. Choosing **one annual event** builds goodwill.

## After-school clubs and activities

| Type | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Bukatsu** (club activities) | Junior high intensity high |
| **After-school care** (*gakudo*) | Until parents finish work |
| **Cram school** (*juku*) | Common academically |

Elementary clubs lighter than junior high. Commute and dinner schedule shift in junior high.

## Japanese classroom culture differences

| Value | Classroom sign |
| --- | --- |
| **Group harmony** | Group cleaning (*osoji*) |
| **Respect** | Bowing, quiet corridors |
| **Self-regulation** | Lunch serving rotation |
| **Uniform rules** | Hair color policies strict in some schools |
| **No shoes indoors** | Indoor shoes (*uwabaki*) |

Discipline is often **social expectation**, not only teacher punishment.

## International school realities and costs

| Topic | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **Tuition** | Often ¥1.5 million to ¥3 million+ per year |
| **Application** | Assessments, waiting lists |
| **Bus** | Extra fee |
| **Curriculum** | IB, British, American, etc. |

Good for **3-year assignment** families. Expensive for multi-child long stays unless employer subsidy.

## Common mistakes foreign parents make

- Enrolling child before **address registration** complete
- Assuming **English teacher** exists daily at public school
- Ignoring **allergy forms** until first lunch week
- Missing **April paperwork** window
- **Comparing** Japanese school only to home country loudly at PTA
- Pulling child from school for long trips without **formal absence** process
- Not saving **ward education desk** phone number

## FAQ

### Can foreign children attend public school for free?

Tuition is free for compulsory stages; lunches, trips, and supplies cost money.

### Do we need fluent Japanese as parents?

No, but school communication is mostly Japanese. Use translation and ward support.

### Can we choose any public school?

Usually zoned school. Exceptions need approval.

### Is April enrollment mandatory?

Strong preference. Mid-year enrollment possible with coordination.

### Are international schools accredited for US universities?

Major schools yes. Verify accreditation (IB, WASC, etc.).

### What if my child does not speak Japanese?

JSL support and patience. Younger children adapt faster.

### Is bullying a bigger issue?

Bullying exists as anywhere. Report to school early via homeroom teacher.

### Can we homeschool in Japan?

Regulated; rules strict. Research municipal and national requirements separately.

### Do schools accept short-term visa children?

Depends on municipality and visa. Ask ward education section.

### What documents for enrollment?

Residence card, registration proof, passport, vaccination record, prior school records if any.
