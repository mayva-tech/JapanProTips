## Why vending machines are everywhere in Japan

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **High foot traffic** | Stations, offices, schools |
| **Low vandalism in many areas** | Machines stay outdoors |
| **Beverage-focused retail model** | Drinks are core product |
| **Trust in cashless adoption** | IC and QR common |
| **24-hour need** | After konbini hours |
| **Land use** | Small footprint on streets |

Density is **practical**, not a theme park exhibit.

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Treating every machine as a tourist attraction. For residents, it is often the **fastest drink on the walk home**.

## Cash, IC cards, and QR payments

| Method | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Coins and bills** | Still common |
| **Transport IC (Suica, PASMO)** | Tap reader on machine |
| **Credit tap** | Newer machines |
| **PayPay / d Barai QR** | Scan screen code |

Payments: [mobile payments](/residents/japan-mobile-payment-guide). IC: [IC card Japan](/residents/ic-card-japan). Suica: [Suica and PASMO](/guides/suica-pasmo-guide).

| Payment example | Flow |
| --- | --- |

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Tapping IC before choosing drink on some models. Read **select first or tap first** on sticker.

## FAQ

### Can I use Suica on vending machines?

Most drink machines in cities accept **transport IC**. Look for the IC logo.

### Are vending machines cheaper than konbini?

Often **similar**. Speed is the main win, not big savings.

### Can I drink on the train?

Varies. **No alcohol** on many lines. Closed drinks in bag are common. Avoid rush hour open cups.

### What is the most popular resident drink?

**Cold barley tea and green tea** in summer, **hot coffee** in winter. Personal taste varies.

### Do machines take 1000 yen bills?

Many do, with change. **Coins faster** at busy times.

### Why are some slots empty?

Sold out or **restock pending**. Try next machine or konbini.

### Are specialty food machines worth it?

Fun once. **Drink machines** cover daily needs.

### Is tap water better than buying bottles?

Tap is safe in Japan. Bottles are **convenience**, not necessity.
