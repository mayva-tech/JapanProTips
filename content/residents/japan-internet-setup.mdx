## Why internet setup in Japan can be confusing

Japanese apartments often share one fiber line for the whole building. Management may require a specific provider or forbid wall drilling. Contracts can run two years with fees for early exit.

- The listing ad rarely tells you which fiber port is live in your unit.
- Romaji building names on forms must match your lease exactly.
- Installers speak Japanese; appointments need someone home for hours.
- Pocket Wi-Fi is easy short term but costly as a permanent home solution.

<InfoBox title="Watch for this">
  Ask the landlord or management company which internet types are allowed before you sign a provider contract online.
</InfoBox>

## The main types of internet in Japan

Most residents pick one primary path for home use and keep mobile data as backup.

### Fiber internet

**Fiber** (光回線 / hikari) is wired broadband to the building or unit. It is the default choice when your apartment supports it.

- Speed is high and stable for video calls and large downloads.
- Install requires a technician visit or pre-wired activation.
- Building type (mansion vs family house) changes the install style.

### Pocket Wi-Fi

**Pocket Wi-Fi** is a portable router on a mobile network. You order online and receive a device by mail.

- Setup is fast with no install appointment.
- Data caps and speed vary by plan and area congestion.
- Good for the first weeks; expensive for heavy month-round use at home.

### Home wireless internet

**Home wireless** (home 5G or LTE routers) uses mobile networks without fiber cable to the room.

- Useful when fiber is blocked or the port is inactive.
- Performance depends on signal at your window.
- Some plans market themselves as fiber alternatives in rural towns.

### Mobile tethering

**Tethering** from your phone plan can cover light browsing short term.

- Fine for one person on basic tasks, not a household default.
- Heat and battery drain on the phone add up quickly.
- Check fair-use rules on your mobile contract before relying on it daily.

<TipBox title="Field note">
  Run a speed test on mobile data in the room where you will work before you skip fiber entirely.
</TipBox>

## Apartment internet compatibility

Not every building allows every provider. Check three layers: building infrastructure, your unit port, and management rules.

- **FTTH** readiness: fiber terminal in the closet or media panel.
- **Provider lock-in**: some mansions contract one vendor for the whole tower.
- **Rental furniture** units may forbid screws or new holes.
- New towers sometimes show fiber on ads while individual ports are still dark.

## Mansion type vs family type internet

**Mansion type** serves apartment buildings with shared vertical wiring. **Family type** (戸建て) serves detached houses with a drop to the property.

| Style | Typical building | Install feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mansion | Concrete tower, shared shaft | Technician may only activate indoor terminal |
| Family | House, small wooden apartment | Outdoor line work possible |

- Wrong type selected online delays install by weeks.
- Management fax numbers appear on mansion forms; houses use owner contact.
- Confirm which label your lease uses before checkout.

## Common internet providers in Japan

Major brands include NTT-related fiber, cable companies, and mobile carriers that resell fiber bundles. No single name wins everywhere.

- Compare total monthly cost, not only the promo first-six-month price.
- Bundled mobile discounts matter if you already use that carrier.
- English phone support varies; some resellers target foreign residents.
- Read building compatibility lists on each site after you enter your address.

<InfoBox>
  This guide does not rank providers. Enter your postal code on two or three official sites and see who returns green checkmarks for your building.
</InfoBox>

## Router basics and Wi-Fi setup

Fiber installs often include a **ONU/modem** and optional **Wi-Fi router**. You can rent from the provider or buy your own compatible unit.

- Place the router central, elevated, and away from metal closets.
- 2.4 GHz reaches far; 5 GHz is faster nearby.
- Change default Wi-Fi passwords on day one.
- Label the SSID if many similar names appear in dense buildings.

## Installation timelines and appointments

Fiber activation commonly takes **two to four weeks** after application when slots are open. Busy seasons stretch longer.

- Choose install windows where you can be home four hours.
- Technicians confirm port location, test speed, and hand you paperwork.
- Mansion installs may finish faster if the line is already live two floors down.
- Rain and typhoon weeks reschedule outdoor work on houses.

## Typical costs and contract lengths

Expect a monthly fee plus optional router rental, construction fee waivers, or cashback campaigns.

| Cost type | What residents see |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly service | Varies by speed tier and bundles |
| Construction / install | Often discounted in campaigns |
| Router rental | Small monthly add-on |
| Contract term | Commonly two years on promo plans |

- Cashback deals may require staying the full term.
- Read what happens to price after the promo months end.

## Cancellation fees and moving apartments

Early cancellation can trigger **remaining month penalties** or equipment return fees. Moving within Japan may allow a transfer instead of a full cancel.

- Notify the provider before you move; transfer rules differ.
- Return rented routers with the provided shipping label.
- If the new apartment uses a locked provider, you may need to close and reopen.
- Update your service address for billing and technician visits.

## What foreign residents should prepare

- Japanese address in the exact lease format
- Residence card and phone number
- Building management contact for mansion installs
- A friend or translator for the install window if needed
- Tape or floor mat if technicians work indoors (slippers culture)

## Common mistakes residents make

- Applying for family-type fiber in a mansion tower
- Buying a router before confirming ONU port type
- Scheduling install before management approves wall access
- Keeping pocket Wi-Fi for a year at home office usage levels
- Ignoring contract end dates when switching mobile carriers

## Simple internet setup checklist

- Confirm building allows fiber and which providers
- Enter address on provider sites and screenshot compatibility
- Pick fiber, home wireless, or short-term pocket Wi-Fi for week one
- Book install or order SIM router with delivery to your address
- Set Wi-Fi password and run a speed test at your desk
- File management approval paper if the form requests it
- Save contract PDF and support phone in one folder

## Final advice

Treat internet like housing: verify the building first, then choose the plan. A free router promo matters less than a line that actually reaches your room.

Start with a backup: mobile data or pocket Wi-Fi for the gap between move-in and install day. Most frustration comes from assuming fiber is live because the listing said so.
