## Why Japanese drugstores feel different from other countries

| Abroad habit | Japan drugstore reality |
| --- | --- |
| **Pharmacy sells mostly medicine** | Wide floors: beauty, snacks, cleaning |
| **Cosmetics in department stores** | Drugstore serums are mainstream daily use |
| **Supermarket for household goods** | Drugstore competes on small packs |
| **Late-night only at convenience store** | Many drugstores open until 22:00+ |
| **Seasonal aisle is small** | Full rotation: pollen, heat, rain |

Drugstores here are **dense life-support retail**, not just pills.

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Thinking drugstores are only for tourists buying masks. Residents run **weekly maintenance trips** the same way others use Target or Boots.

## FAQ

### Which drugstore chain is best for residents?

The best chain is **nearest your home or station**. Layout familiarity beats brand loyalty for most people.

### Can residents buy medicine without a prescription?

Many **OTC** products yes. Prescription drugs need a Japanese prescription filled at pharmacy counter.

### Are drugstores cheaper than supermarkets?

**Food** usually no. **OTC sales, skincare sets, cleaning trial sizes** often competitive on sale days.

### Do I need a point card?

Not required. Free cards pay off if you use the same chain monthly.

### Can I ask the pharmacist in English?

Sometimes in central Tokyo. Elsewhere use translation app and symptom notes.

### Do drugstores sell ward-specific trash bags?

Sometimes. Stock varies. Confirm bag name with [trash guide](/residents/japan-trash-recycling-guide).

### Is tourist tax-free shopping relevant for residents?

Generally **no**. Tax-free counters target short-term visitors. Residents pay standard tax.

### What should I buy in my first month?

Toilet paper, detergent, bathroom mold prevention, sunscreen, basic pain reliever, bandages, and one skincare basic you will actually use.
