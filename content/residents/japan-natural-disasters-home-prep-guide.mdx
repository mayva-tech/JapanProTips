## The reality of living with earthquakes in Japan

Japan records many small quakes. **Damaging quakes are infrequent** in any one place, but building codes and daily habits assume they can happen.

| Fact | Resident meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Frequent small shakes** | Normal, practice your plan |
| **Building standards improved** | Post-1981 and newer often stronger |
| **Train stops after big shake** | Plan walk or wait |
| **Aftershocks** | Can follow main quake |
| **Utilities may stop** | Short outages possible |

| Building age | General note |
| --- | --- |
| Newer tower | Seismic design, management plans |
| 1970s-1980s wood | Check landlord retrofit info |
| Low-rise wood | Furniture tip risk higher |

You cannot eliminate risk. You can **reduce injury and confusion**.

## Typhoons and heavy rain explained realistically

**Typhoons (台風)** bring wind and rain over **1-3 days**. Tracks change. Forecasts improve 48-72 hours out.

| Phase | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| **Forecast** | Time to shop and secure balcony |
| **Approach** | Wind, rain bands, train delays |
| **Passage** | Peak rain, possible flooding low areas |
| **Exit** | Cleanup, garbage backlog |

| Heavy rain without typhoon | Risk |
| --- | --- |
| **Guerrilla downpours** | Sudden flooding underpasses |
| **Landslide warnings** | Hillside neighborhoods |
| **River rise** | Low areas near rivers |

Rainy season context: [rainy season guide](/guides/japan-rainy-season-guide). Seasons: [weather living guide](/residents/japan-weather-living-guide).

<InfoBox title="Foreign residents panic about this">
  **"Every typhoon is a catastrophe in Tokyo."** Many typhoons bring **rain and transit delays**, not building collapse. Still prepare balcony items and food. Match prep to forecast level.
</InfoBox>

## Furniture anchoring and apartment safety

Most home injuries in quakes come from **falls and cuts**, not collapsing buildings.

| Item | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Tall shelves** | L-bracket to wall if landlord allows |
| **TV** | Strap or low stand |
| **Microwave** | Non-slip mat |
| **Fridge** | Brake wheels if any |
| **Glass doors** | Film reduces shatter |
| **Bed position** | Not under heavy shelf |

| Product | Where to buy |
| --- | --- |
| **転倒防止** kits | Home center, Amazon |
| **Non-slip pads** | 100 yen shop |
| **Shatter film** | Home center |

**Scenario:** 1DK in Chiba, bookshelf anchored with two brackets. After M4 shake, books stayed. Unanchored lamp fell in neighbor unit.

## What to do during earthquakes

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Indoors, small shake** | Pause, look up for fall risk |
| **Strong shake** | Drop, cover head, hold table leg if safe |
| **Kitchen** | Turn off stove if you can safely |
| **Near window** | Move away from glass |
| **Elevator** | Do not use during and after |
| **After shaking stops** | Shoes on, check gas, exit if smell |
| **Outdoors** | Away from signs and walls |

| After | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Water, gas, breaker | Off if damage |
| Phone alerts | Aftershock info |
| Building damage | Cracks, water leaks |
| Neighbors | Knock if safe |

## FAQ

### Do I need a full disaster bag in Tokyo?

A small home stock plus grab pouch is enough for most urban residents. Rural or hillside areas need more.

### How much water should I store?

Many guides suggest about 3 liters per person per day for several days. Start with 2 days, expand later.

### Should I tape windows for typhoons?

Film for shatter is more common than tape. Follow building notice. Clear balcony items matter more.

### Is my new apartment earthquake safe?

Newer buildings follow modern codes. Ask management for building info. Prepare furniture inside anyway.

### What apps should I use?

Safety tips, Yahoo Disaster, municipal apps. See emergency guide for numbers.

### Will the toilet work without power?

Water may stop. Fill tub if warned. Ward may advise portable bags in extreme cases.

### Should I run outside during an earthquake?

Stay inside and cover unless fire or tsunami zone applies. Tsunami zones follow separate maps.

### When do trains stop in typhoons?

Operators announce early suspension. Check apps before commute home.

### Do I need earthquake insurance?

Optional rider on fire insurance. Covers quake fire damage, not all losses. Read policy.

### Can I prepare in a 1R apartment?

Yes. Under-bed water, wall anchor one shelf, headlamp, small food box.

### What if I do not read Japanese alerts?

Enable phone emergency language, use bilingual apps, ask neighbor once for building group LINE.

### How often should I replace food?

Check dates every 6 months. Eat items nearing expiry and rebuy.
