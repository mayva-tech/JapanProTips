## Why taxes in Japan confuse foreign residents

Common confusion points:

- **Two layers**: national income tax plus **resident tax** (*juminzei*)
- **Timing**: resident tax often billed **after** the income year
- **Japanese envelopes** with payment slips and no English summary
- **Withholding** on salary that still does not cover everything
- Mixing **health insurance** and **pension** with "tax" mentally

A simple map helps: payroll handles part, you or your employer file part, your city sends bills for local tax.

## The main taxes residents encounter

| Tax / payment | Who collects | Typical payer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Income tax** (national) | National Tax Agency via employer or self-filing | Employee, freelancer |
| **Resident tax** (local) | City or ward | You (often by slip) |
| **Consumption tax** (VAT) | Included in prices | Everyone at register |
| **NHI premiums** | Municipality | Separate from income tax |
| **Pension contributions** | Japan Pension Service | Separate from income tax |

This guide focuses on **income tax** and **resident tax**. NHI and pension are related paperwork but not the same line item.

## Income tax explained simply

**Income tax** is national tax on your earnings and some other income for the calendar year (January to December).

| Concept | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Taxable income** | Salary minus deductions and exemptions |
| **Progressive rates** | Higher income, higher marginal rate |
| **Withholding** | Employer takes tax from monthly pay |
| **Tax return** | Form you file if extra tax is due or refund owed |

Most employees meet income tax mainly through **payroll withholding** and **year-end adjustment**, not a full return every year.

## Resident tax explained simply

**Resident tax** (*juminzei*) funds your city and prefecture: schools, fire services, local programs.

| Part | Share (typical) |
| --- | --- |
| **Municipal** | About 10% of prior year taxable income (rate set locally) |
| **Prefectural** | About 4% of prior year taxable income |

Often quoted together as roughly **10% total** on taxable income after basic adjustments. Exact calculation uses municipal rules.

**Key idea:** Resident tax for calendar year **2025** is generally based on income from **2024**, billed from **June 2025** onward.

## Why resident tax shocks new arrivals

<InfoBox title="New residents panic about this">
  You work all of 2024. Payroll withholds **income tax**. In **June 2025** you receive **resident tax slips** for thousands of yen per month. That is normal. Resident tax was usually **not** fully withheld monthly unless your employer set up special monthly collection.
</InfoBox>

| Situation | What you feel |
| --- | --- |
| First job mid-year | Partial year, confusing slips |
| High June bill | Prior year income catching up |
| Moved cities | Bills may come from old and new municipalities during transition |

**Example:** ¥4,000,000 annual taxable income mindset. Resident tax might land around **¥400,000/year** split across monthly payments from June to May (rough illustration, not your exact number).

## National health insurance relationship

**NHI** premiums are **not** income tax. They are separate municipal bills based on prior income and household.

- Enrolled at ward office after registration
- Premium notices arrive on their own schedule
- Non-payment affects insurance status, not only tax records

Guide: [National Health Insurance for residents](/residents/japan-health-insurance).

| Item | Income / resident tax | NHI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collected by | National + city tax office | Municipality |
| Shown on payslip sometimes | Tax withholding | Rarely full NHI |
| Uses My Number | Yes | Yes |

## Pension deductions explained

**Pension** contributions are also separate from income tax, though they affect take-home pay.

| Employee | Freelancer / national pension |
| --- | --- |
| **Kosei nenkin** via employer split | **Kokumin nenkin** invoices |
| Deducted on payslip | Paid by payment slips |

Pension may reduce taxable income in some calculations. Do not confuse pension payment with having "paid all taxes."

Guide: [Japan pension system](/residents/japan-pension-system).

## Tax withholding from salaries

Employers estimate tax and deduct monthly from salary.

| Payslip line (examples) | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **所得税** (*shotokuzei*) | Income tax withholding |
| **社会保険** | Social insurance (health, pension employee share) |
| **雇用保険** | Employment insurance |

**Example monthly picture (illustrative only):**

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Gross salary | ¥300,000 |
| Social insurance | -¥45,000 |
| Income tax withholding | -¥8,000 |
| Resident tax (if monthly collection set) | -¥25,000 |
| Net pay | About ¥222,000 |

Numbers vary widely. Ask HR for your **源泉徴収票** (*gensen choshuhyo*) yearly slip.

## Freelancers vs employees

| | **Employee** | **Freelancer / self-employed** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Income tax** | Withholding + year-end adjustment often enough | **Final tax return** usually required |
| **Resident tax** | Slips from city; may self-pay | Slips from city; estimate payments possible |
| **Invoices** | Payslip | **Qualified invoice** (*invoice system*) rules may apply |
| **Record keeping** | Employer tracks | You track expenses and receipts |

Freelancers should plan **accounting software or a tax professional** early.

## Tax returns in Japan

**Kakutei shinkoku** (final tax return) filed with the tax office when required or beneficial.

You may need to file if:

- Freelancer or multiple employers
- Side income over reporting thresholds
- Large medical deductions or housing loan credits
- Employer did not year-end adjust
- Want refund from over-withholding

Filing window for most individuals: **mid-February to mid-March** for prior calendar year.

Online filing exists with **My Number** linkage for some users.

## Year-end adjustment explained

**Nenmatsu chosei** (year-end adjustment) is when employers recalculate annual tax for employees in **December**.

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| You submit | Dependents, insurance, housing loan papers |
| Employer calculates | Annual income tax due |
| December pay | Extra withholding or refund on last salary |
| January | **Withholding slip** available for records |

If you forget to submit forms, you may over-withhold or underpay and need a return in March.

## Local city tax payment slips

Resident tax often arrives as **payment slips** (*nofusho*) or **special collection** through employer.

| Method | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Bank transfer** | Common auto-debit setup |
| **Konbini payment** | Barcode on slip |
| **Credit card** | Some cities allow |
| **Employer monthly deduction** | Ask HR if available |

Paying bills on time: [pay bills in Japan](/residents/pay-bills-japan).

<TipBox title="Field note">
  Photograph each slip and log due dates in your calendar app the day mail arrives. June is the classic surprise month.
</TipBox>

## Common deadlines and timing

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| **Each payday** | Income tax withholding |
| **December** | Year-end adjustment at work |
| **January** | Withholding certificate from employer |
| **Feb-Mar** | Final tax return if required |
| **June** | Resident tax bills start (prior year income) |
| **June-May (12 months)** | Resident tax installment period typical |

| Calendar year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| **2024** | Earn income |
| **Dec 2024** | Year-end adjustment |
| **Feb-Mar 2025** | Return if needed |
| **Jun 2025-May 2026** | Pay resident tax on 2024 income |

## What happens if you miss payments

| Missed | Possible result |
| --- | --- |
| **Income tax due on return** | Penalties and interest |
| **Resident tax slip** | Late fees, reminders, stronger collection |
| **Repeated non-payment** | Bank account seizure procedures in extreme cases |

If you cannot pay on time, contact the **tax office** or **city tax desk** before ignoring envelopes. Payment plans sometimes exist.

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Assuming **payslip withholding** covers **all** resident tax
- Ignoring **June** envelopes as spam
- Not giving **My Number** to employer and getting duplicate confusion
- Missing **year-end adjustment** forms in December
- Mixing **NHI** bills with income tax and paying wrong slip
- **Freelance income** unreported until a letter arrives
- Not updating **address** after move (bills go to wrong ward)

## Practical budgeting advice

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Save **~10% of gross** in a separate account | Resident tax buffer |
| Ask HR about **monthly resident tax collection** | Smoother cash flow |
| Keep **withholding slip** PDF each January | Visa renewal, loans |
| Track **side income** monthly if freelancing | March filing easier |
| Use bank **auto-transfer** for city tax | Avoid konbini late fees |

**Example budget:** Net take-home ¥250,000/month. Set aside ¥25,000 in a "city tax" sub-account from June to May if employer does not deduct resident tax monthly.

## FAQ

### Do all foreign residents pay the same tax?

No. Income level, visa status, treaties, and deductions change outcomes.

### What is resident tax vs income tax?

Income tax is national. Resident tax is local, based largely on prior year income.

### Why is my June pay lower?

Resident tax collection may start. Check payslip lines.

### Do I file a tax return every year?

Many employees do not. Freelancers and complex cases usually do.

### Can I pay resident tax at konbini?

Often yes, using the barcode slip from your city.

### Does leaving Japan end resident tax?

You may have a final settlement or departure procedures. Notify municipality.

### Is consumption tax the same as income tax?

No. Consumption tax is 10% on goods and services at purchase.

### Where do I get help in English?

Some wards have foreign resident desks. Tax offices vary. Licensed tax accountants (*zeirishi*) help for a fee.

### Does My Number share everything publicly?

No. Used for authorized administrative matching.

### Are pension and health insurance tax deductible?

Some amounts affect year-end adjustment and returns. Keep official certificates.
