## What the Shinkansen actually is

The Shinkansen is Japan's **high-speed rail network** for long city-to-city trips. Tourists call it the bullet train. Locals call it Shinkansen, or sometimes "Shink" in casual speech.

- Runs on **dedicated tracks** separate from commuter trains.
- Stops at major city stations (Tokyo, Shin-Osaka, Kyoto, Hiroshima, Sendai, and many more).
- Uses **named train types** (Nozomi, Hikari, Kodama, Hayabusa) that differ in speed and stops.
- Feels more like a short flight without security: assigned platforms, car lines on the ground, quiet cars.

It is **not** every fast train in Japan. Metro lines in Tokyo, private railways around Fuji, and many regional expresses are different systems with different tickets.

## Why tourists get confused

Most panic comes from mixing three worlds at once: **local trains**, **seat reservations**, and **pass products** (JR Pass, regional passes).

<InfoBox title="What tourists panic about">
  - "I tapped my IC card but the gate beeped." IC cards often do not cover long Shinkansen legs by themselves.
  - "Google Maps says Nozomi but I have a JR Pass." Many passes exclude Nozomi unless you pay extra.
  - "My ticket says car 7, seat 12C but the platform screen shows something else." You are probably reading the local train screen.
  - "Everyone is running." Commuters run. Shinkansen passengers usually walk to a marked line and wait.
</InfoBox>

<TipBox title="Beginner reassurance">
  Missing one train is rarely a disaster. Stations sell later seats, sometimes standing non-reserved, or the next departure 15-30 minutes out on busy corridors.
</TipBox>

<OperationalWarning noteId="shinkansen-ic-ticket-stack">
  <p>
    IC tap habits from metro days do not replace a Shinkansen ticket stack when the
    leg needs a seat reservation or express surcharge. If the gate tone is wrong,
    pause with paper in hand before you argue with a line behind you.
  </p>
</OperationalWarning>

## Types of Shinkansen trains explained

Train **names** are service levels, not separate companies you must memorize. On most first trips you will see these:

| Train | Corridor (typical) | Speed / stops | First-timer note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nozomi** | Tokaido-Sanyo (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, Hiroshima) | Fastest, fewer stops | Often excluded on standard JR Pass |
| **Hikari** | Tokaido-Sanyo | Faster than Kodama, more stops than Nozomi | Common JR Pass choice |
| **Kodama** | Tokaido-Sanyo | Slowest, many stops | Cheaper, good if you are not in a rush |
| **Hayabusa** | Tohoku / Hokkaido link | Very fast northbound service | Seat reservation strongly recommended |

### Nozomi

- **Fewest stops**, shortest Tokyo to Kyoto time on Tokaido-Sanyo.
- Popular with individual ticket buyers who want speed.
- **Check JR Pass rules** before you board. Standard national pass usually needs a supplement or a different train.

### Hikari

- Sensible default when you want speed without Nozomi pass limits.
- Still fast for Golden Route trips.
- Book reserved seats on peak weekends.

### Kodama

- Stops at more stations. Trip takes longer.
- Fine when you leave midday and are not racing the clock.
- Easier to find non-reserved space off-peak on some legs (still check train length).

### Hayabusa

- Main **northbound** high-speed name tourists see for Sendai, Morioka, Shin-Aomori, and Hokkaido links.
- Winter weather can affect schedules. Build buffer on ski or north trips.

## Reserved vs non-reserved seats

Shinkansen cars are split into **reserved** (numbered seat) and **non-reserved** (unassigned seat in designated cars).

| | Reserved | Non-reserved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seat | Assigned row and letter | First open seat in non-reserved cars |
| Peak travel | Strongly recommended | Can fill early |
| Peace of mind | High | Lower |
| Price | Higher base fare | Lower base fare |
| Best for | First-timers, luggage, families | Flexible off-peak travelers |

<TipBox title="Beginner reassurance">
  For your **first** Shinkansen ride, buy **reserved**. The small extra cost buys silence when you are still decoding signs.
</TipBox>

**How to read your ticket:** Look for car number (**sharyo**), seat (**zaseki**), train name, and date. Match car number to platform floor decals.

<WhatPeopleMiss noteId="shinkansen-floor-decals">
  <p>
    Platform screens list many trains. Tourist stress spikes when the name matches
    but the departure minute differs. Trust the departure row, car count, and
    floor decals together, not one glowing line in isolation.
  </p>
</WhatPeopleMiss>

## Green Car explained

Green Car is **business class** on Shinkansen: wider seats, more legroom, quieter cabin, higher price.

- Requires a **Green seat ticket** or Green pass product. A standard reserved ticket is not enough.
- Great for long legs if you value comfort and work space.
- Not required for a normal first trip. Ordinary reserved cars are already clean and fast.

<InfoBox title="What tourists panic about">
  Walking into Green Car with a standard ticket. Conductors check. You pay the difference or move to ordinary cars if space exists.
</InfoBox>

## How to buy tickets

You can buy Shinkansen tickets in several ways. Pick one path and stay consistent until you are comfortable.

### Station machines

- JR ticket machines in major stations sell Shinkansen seats.
- English mode exists on many machines. Go slow, confirm train **name** and **time**.
- Pay cash or card as the machine allows.
- You receive **paper tickets** (and sometimes a seat card). Keep both until you exit.

### Ticket counters

- **Midori no Madoguchi** (JR green window) and travel service centers handle complex trips.
- Best for: first ride, oversized luggage slots, JR Pass seat reservations, and when machines stress you out.
- Say your destination, date, time preference, and **reserved or non-reserved**.
- Helpful phrase: **"Kono densha no zaseki wo onegaishimasu"** (a seat on this train, please).

### SmartEX and online booking

- **SmartEX** (and other official online channels) sell e-tickets for many Tokaido-Sanyo and related services.
- Register before travel, link payment, choose train and seat on a map.
- You receive a **QR or reference** to pass gates at some stations. Rules vary by station hardware.
- Strong for travelers who already know their train time.

<TipBox title="Field note">
  If you hold a **JR Pass**, you still need **seat reservations** for many trains. The pass is not always a walk-on ticket for a numbered seat.
</TipBox>

## How to enter the station gates

Shinkansen travel usually starts after you enter the **Shinkansen ticket gate** area inside the station, not the metro gates downstairs.

1. Find signs for **Shinkansen** (often in English).
2. Insert **both** tickets if the machine asks (base fare slip plus seat slip, when you have two).
3. Walk through. Do not linger in the gate.
4. On exit at your destination, insert tickets again. The gate keeps them.

**IC card only:** Many long legs still need a **paper seat ticket** unless you bought a product that is fully IC-compatible. If the gate rejects your IC tap, you probably need a paper combo.

Use [Suica and PASMO explained](/guides/suica-pasmo-guide) for local segments before and after the bullet train.

## How platforms work

Once inside the Shinkansen concourse:

1. Find your **train line** on the departure board (train name, time, platform).
2. Go to the platform early. Trains board only a few minutes before departure.
3. Stand on the **car number** painted on the platform matching your ticket.
4. Let exiting passengers off before you step in.
5. Find your row and seat, or a non-reserved car if that is what you bought.

<InfoBox title="What tourists panic about">
  Standing at the wrong car line. The train is long. Walking inside with huge luggage against the flow is miserable. Check platform decals 10 minutes before boarding.
</InfoBox>

## Luggage rules and oversized baggage

Shinkansen luggage rules tightened for **very large bags**. Planning beats surprises at the gate.

| Bag size (JR guidance mindset) | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Within carry-on limits | Store in overhead rack or behind last seat row |
| Over size thresholds | Book **oversized baggage space** on many trains when required |
| Multiple huge suitcases | Consider [luggage forwarding](/guides/japan-luggage-shipping) between cities |

- Pack a **small day bag** at your seat. Aisle access matters.
- Lift bags overhead before the train fills. Ask staff if you need help.
- Do not block aisles during the ride.

## Food, ekiben, and etiquette onboard

Eating on Shinkansen is normal and part of the fun.

- Buy **ekiben** (station lunch boxes) on the concourse before boarding.
- Drinks from vending machines on board or from konbini beforehand.
- Avoid strong-smelling food in quiet seasons if the car is packed.
- Take trash with you or use the bag on your tray.

**Onboard etiquette:**

- Phone calls in the aisle are discouraged. Use messaging.
- Quiet conversation is fine. Volume stays low.
- Recline gently. Space is tighter than airlines in ordinary cars.
- Shoes stay on unless you are in a very informal group (not a rule, just social norm).

## Common mistakes first-timers make

- Going to the **wrong station** (Tokyo vs Shinagawa vs Ueno) for their ticket.
- Boarding **Nozomi** with a pass that excludes it.
- Entering **metro gates** with Shinkansen tickets (or the reverse).
- Losing the **exit ticket** and delaying exit.
- Sitting in **reserved seat 8A** when they bought non-reserved.
- Forgetting that **last trains** exist but are not 24 hours on all corridors.
- Storing luggage in the aisle during boarding rush.

<TipBox title="Beginner reassurance">
  Station staff expect questions. Show your ticket and say your city name. They will point, not scold.
</TipBox>

## Tokyo to Kyoto example walkthrough

A realistic first Shinkansen trip many visitors take:

| Step | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Decide date and morning vs afternoon (Hikari or Nozomi if buying individual tickets). |
| 2 | Buy **reserved** ticket Tokyo to Kyoto Station, or reserve with JR Pass at a counter. |
| 3 | Arrive **Shinagawa, Tokyo, or Shin-Yokohama** as your ticket shows (do not assume "Tokyo" without reading). |
| 4 | Enter **Shinkansen gates** with paper tickets. |
| 5 | Find platform on board, match **car 6** (example) on ground decals. |
| 6 | Board, store bag overhead, eat ekiben, enjoy ~2+ hours depending on train. |
| 7 | Exit Kyoto gates. Use IC card for city subway or bus. |

<InfoBox title="What tourists panic about">
  "Kyoto Station" vs "Kyoto" on apps. Your Shinkansen ticket usually says **Kyoto** (the main JR station). Local maps may label subway stops differently.
</InfoBox>

## Is the JR Pass needed?

Not automatically. The pass is a bundle for many JR rides, not a requirement to ride Shinkansen.

- **Tokyo only:** Pass rarely needed for Shinkansen.
- **Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka loop:** Run the math. Individual tickets often win after the 2023 price hike.
- **Wide multi-city loop in 7-14 days:** Pass may win.

## Shinkansen etiquette

- Line up behind platform marks. Do not crowd the door before passengers exit.
- Keep voices low. Many riders sleep or work.
- Do not leave luggage in the aisle.
- If you have a reserved seat, sit there even if an empty non-reserved car looks tempting.
- Thank staff with a small nod if they help with luggage.

## FAQ

### Is the Shinkansen hard for first-time visitors?

No. It is structured. One careful ride teaches the pattern for the rest of the trip.

### Can I use Suica for Shinkansen?

Sometimes for some products, but many first-timers still use **paper seat tickets** for long legs. Do not assume IC alone works until you confirm your fare type.

### How early should I arrive?

**15-20 minutes** is a sane minimum at big stations. More on holidays and Sunday evening peaks.

### Nozomi vs Hikari for Tokyo to Kyoto?

**Nozomi** is faster. **Hikari** is still quick and often better if you use a JR Pass that excludes Nozomi.

### Do I need a reservation?

Strongly recommended for first-timers and peak dates. Non-reserved works off-peak when you accept uncertainty.

### Can I eat on the train?

Yes. Ekiben is normal. Keep mess low and take trash with you.

### Where does luggage go?

Overhead racks and space behind the last row in many cars. Oversized bags may need a booked slot.

### What if I miss my train?

Talk to station staff. You usually rebook on a later train, paying fare difference rules as applicable.

### Is Green Car worth it?

Nice for comfort. Ordinary reserved is already excellent for a first trip.
