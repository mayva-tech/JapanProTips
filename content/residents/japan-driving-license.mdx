## Can foreigners drive in Japan?

Many foreign residents drive legally for a limited period with an international driving permit (IDP) or under bilateral agreements. Long-term residents usually need a Japanese license or a formal conversion.

- Tourist-style IDP use has strict time limits tied to entry status.
- Conversion replaces your foreign license with a Japanese one for daily driving.
- Car rental and insurance paperwork often ask for a domestic-format license.
- Driving without a valid license carries serious penalties.

<InfoBox title="Watch for this">
  Confirm your status with the licensing center for your prefecture before you buy a car or sign a long lease in a rural area.
</InfoBox>

## International driving permit vs license conversion

An **IDP** is a translation booklet used with your home license for short stays. **Conversion** (外国免許切替) is the process to obtain a Japanese license while residing in Japan.

| Path | Typical use | Long-term resident? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IDP plus home license | Short visits, some early weeks | Usually temporary only |
| License conversion | Daily driving after settling in | Common goal for residents |
| Full driving school | No foreign license to convert | Separate long route |

- IDP rules depend on your country and Japan's agreements.
- Conversion assumes you already hold a valid foreign license with enough history.
- Keep both concepts separate when reading forum posts.

## Countries with easier conversion agreements

Japan has agreements with some countries that reduce testing. Others face knowledge and road tests. The list changes; verify on official pages for your nationality.

- Some countries skip the driving test but still require documents and a knowledge check.
- Others require full testing at the prefectural center.
- Where the license was **issued** can matter as much as your passport country.
- Embassy sites and prefecture PDFs are safer than old blog charts.

<TipBox title="Field note">
  Screenshot the official eligibility table for your country on the day you apply. Do not rely on a friend from a different nationality.
</TipBox>

## The basic conversion process

The flow repeats nationwide with local details. Most residents visit the **driver's license center** (運転免許センター) for their area.

1. Confirm you are eligible and gather documents.
2. Obtain an official translation if required.
3. Book or join the queue for center intake.
4. Pass knowledge and/or driving tests when required.
5. Pay fees, take a photo, and receive a temporary paper until the card arrives.

Timelines stretch when appointment slots are full. Plan weeks, not days, in busy prefectures.

## Required documents

Centers publish checklists. Arrive with originals and copies as instructed. Missing one paper can end the visit.

### Passport

Valid **passport** proves identity and travel history when staff ask about license issue dates abroad.

- Bring the physical book, not only a phone image.
- Old passports may be requested if stamps show residency abroad.

### Residence card

Your **residence card** (在留カード) shows status and registered address.

- Address on the card should match municipal records.
- Update the card before conversion if you moved recently.

### Current license

Present your valid **foreign license**. Staff check issue date, expiry, and categories.

- Bring every renewal version if the center asks for history.
- Temporary paper licenses from abroad may be rejected; confirm locally.

### Official translation

Many countries need a **JAF translation** or other approved translation of the license.

- Translations must match the license text exactly.
- Stickers and unofficial printouts are not substitutes unless the center says so.

### Proof of residence period

Some applicants must show they lived in the license-issuing country for a minimum period after the license was granted.

- School transcripts, employment records, or exit stamps may help.
- Rules differ by agreement; ask the center which proof they accept.

## JAF translation basics

**JAF** (Japan Automobile Federation) is the common source for license translations into Japanese.

- Apply with a clear scan or copy per JAF instructions.
- Processing takes several days to weeks depending on volume.
- Fees are modest compared to retesting; budget the wait before you book the center.
- Keep the original translation sheet with your license folder.

<InfoBox>
  Other approved translators exist for some licenses. If your embassy names an alternative, confirm the center accepts it before you pay.
</InfoBox>

## Written and driving tests

Non-exempt nationalities take a **knowledge test** and sometimes a **road test** on a closed course.

- Knowledge tests use true or false style questions in Japanese or with translation support per center.
- Driving tests check signals, course lanes, and full stops at prescribed lines.
- Automatic-transmission licenses may restrict you to automatic cars in Japan.
- Failing one section usually means a rebook date, not an instant retry.

## Driving center appointments and waiting times

Busy centers use online reservations. Rural centers may allow walk-ins on set days.

- Book the earliest slot when your translation is in hand.
- Morning slots fill first in large cities.
- Rain and holiday weeks slow scheduling.
- Bring snacks; total time on site can exceed half a day even with an appointment.

## Common differences in Japanese driving tests

Course tests reward precision over speed. Examiners watch mirror checks and full stops.

- Stop completely before lines, not rolling stops from abroad.
- Use turn signals before lane changes on the course.
- Check blind spots at marked points even when traffic is empty.
- Stay in the center of narrow lanes; clipping cones fails the run.

## Fees and timelines

Fees include translation, test charges, and license issuance. Amounts vary by prefecture and whether you retest.

| Item | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Translation | One-time JAF or translator fee |
| Application | Prefecture-published center charge |
| Retests | Extra fee per failed section |
| License card | Issuance fee; card arrives by mail later |

- Total cash needs are usually in the tens of thousands of yen, not hundreds.
- Card delivery can take one to two weeks after you pass.
- No honest guide promises a pass date; weather, slots, and test load shift constantly.

## Renewals and expiration basics

Japanese licenses use a gold or green system with renewal lectures on a schedule.

- Mark expiry on your calendar; driving expired is not treated lightly.
- Renewal notices arrive by mail to your registered address.
- Points for violations affect renewal class and lecture length.
- Update your address on the license when you move.

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Driving on IDP after the allowed window closes
- Booking the center before the JAF translation arrives
- Assuming US state rules match another country's agreement
- Forgetting glasses or contacts required on the foreign license
- Practicing only on public roads instead of the course layout

## Simple license conversion checklist

- Confirm agreement rules for your license-issuing country
- Order JAF translation early
- Update residence card address if needed
- Photocopy passport, license, and residence card per center list
- Book the center slot and read the test-day map
- Bring correct glasses, shoes, and cash for fees
- Store the new license paper in the car until the card arrives

## Final advice

Treat conversion like a paperwork sport with one driving exam. Documents done cleanly save more time than last-minute practice laps.

If you fail a section, note what the examiner marked and drill that on the course diagram. Most residents pass on a later slot once they stop driving like they are still at home.
