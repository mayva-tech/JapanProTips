## Why bath culture in Japan surprises foreigners

| Expectation | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **Private bathroom only** | Public bath is normal |
| **Swimsuits** | Nude bathing zone |
| **Quick shower** | Wash seated first, long soak |
| **Spa luxury only** | ¥500 neighborhood sento exists |
| **Tattoos always banned** | Rules softening in some chains |
| **Silent spa** | Chatty local sento sometimes |

Bath culture is **hygiene plus recovery**, not a performance.

## Sento vs onsen vs super sento explained

| Type | Water source | Scale | Typical cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sento (銭湯)** | Heated tap water, sometimes mineral | Neighborhood, simple | ¥500-¥800 |
| **Onsen (温泉)** | Natural hot spring water | Resort, ryokan, day trips | Varies |
| **Super sento (スーパー銭湯)** | Mixed, many pools | Large complex | ¥800-¥2,500+ |
| **Sauna-only / capsule** | N/A | Urban, repeat users | ¥1,500-¥3,000 |

<InfoBox title="Foreign residents misunderstand this">
  **"Sento and onsen are the same."** Sento is a **local public bath house**. Onsen requires **certified natural spring water**. Super sento is a **theme park version** with extras.
</InfoBox>

## Sauna culture in Japan

Sauna culture in cities is **structured rounds**: heat, cool, rest.

| Round pattern | Steps |
| --- | --- |
| **Classic 3-set** | Sauna 8-12 min, cold bath, rest 5 min, repeat |
| **Totono (ととのう)** | Deep rest after heat, chair zone |
| **Mizuburo** | Cold plunge emphasis |

| Sauna type | Feel |
| --- | --- |
| **Dry Finnish** | Hot, wooden room |

| **Salt sauna** | Skin focus |
| **TV sauna (old)** | Less common now |

## The bathing process step-by-step

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pay at front, receive locker key |
| 2 | Remove shoes at entrance |
| 3 | Enter gendered changing room |
| 4 | Undress fully, store clothes in locker |
| 5 | Take small towel into bath area |
| 6 | **Wash body at stool** before tubs |
| 7 | Rinse all soap completely |
| 8 | Enter tub slowly, no diving |
| 9 | Optional sauna round |
| 10 | Rinse, dry, dress, hydrate |

**First-visit scenario:** Resident in Koenji visits sento at 20:00. Pays ¥520, washes thoroughly 10 minutes, soaks 15 minutes, leaves calm instead of scrolling phone at home.

## Washing etiquette explained

Washing is **before** the shared tub, never inside it.

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Sit on stool | Keeps water clean |
| Use shower head or bucket | Rinse soap off |
| No soap in tub | Shared water |
| Tie hair up if long | Hygiene |
| Rinse stool area after | Courtesy |
| Quiet voice | Relaxation space |

| Tool | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Stool | Provided |
| Bucket | Rinse |
| Body soap | Bring or buy on site |
| Shampoo | At wash station only |

### Etiquette quick reference

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Wash first | Jump into tub |
| Keep towel out of tub | Soak towel in water |
| Rinse soap fully | Leave stool soapy |
| Speak softly | Loud phone calls |
| Pat dry before locker | Drip through changing room |

## FAQ

### Do I need to be naked at sento?

Yes in bathing areas. Changing room is also nude culture with small towel.

### Can women use sento?

Yes. Separate women section. Hours are usually same.

### Are tattoos allowed?

Depends on facility. Check policy. Cover stickers work where allowed.

### How long should I stay?

60-90 minutes typical including wash and rest.

### Is sento the same as onsen?

No. Onsen uses natural hot spring water. Sento uses heated bath water.

### Can I bring my phone?

Locker only. Never in bath area.

### What if I cannot read Japanese?

Follow pictures on walls. Watch others. Super sento often has icons.

### Is it weird to go alone?

Very normal. Most people go solo.

### How much cash do I need?

¥1,000 covers many visits with towel rental buffer.

### Can children come?

Yes at many sento. Quiet rules apply.

### Are super sento worth it?

Good for variety, sauna, food. Higher price than local sento.

### Will anyone stare?

Brief glance maybe. Focus on your wash routine, not others.
