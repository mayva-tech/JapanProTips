## Quick answer: Why city hall matters so much in Japan

**Your registered address is the anchor for local life in Japan.** City hall (or your ward office in Tokyo) maintains that record and links you to health insurance, resident tax, voting registration, certificates, and many official notices.

| Without city hall registration | What breaks |
| --- | --- |
| No valid local address on file | Insurance and tax mail go wrong |
| Skipped move notification | Bills split between two cities |
| Lost stamped copies | Banks and landlords ask again |

**Quick answer:** Plan at least one calm morning for your first visit. Bring every ID and housing paper you own.

## What Japanese city halls actually do

| Office type | Who uses it |
| --- | --- |
| **Shiyakusho** (city hall) | Residents of a city |
| **Kuyakusho** (ward office) | Residents of a ward inside a large city |
| **Branch office** | Neighborhoods far from the main building |

You go to the office that **covers your home address**, not your employer's office or a friend's ward.

| City hall role | Real-life effect |
| --- | --- |
| Resident registry | Proof of where you live |
| Local insurance desk | National Health Insurance enrollment |
| Tax and resident tax info | Bills and questions |
| Certificates | *Juminhyo*, tax certificates for banks and visas |
| Family services | Childcare lists, school registration pointers |

<InfoBox title="Most foreign residents misunderstand this">
  - **"Immigration handles everything."** Immigration is national. City hall is municipal.
  - **"I can use any ward office in Tokyo."** You must use the ward where you live.
  - **"One counter does all tasks."** Registration, insurance, and certificates are often separate tickets.
  - **"English is standard."** It varies. Big wards may have a foreign resident desk; small towns may not.
</InfoBox>

## The first city hall visit after moving

| Feeling | What is normal |
| --- | --- |
| Not knowing which button on the ticket machine | Everyone learns once |
| Long wait with no phone entertainment | Bring water, charger |
| Fear of doing something illegal | Staff correct small errors if you stay calm |
| Leaving with a pamphlet stack | Read at home, do not panic same day |

**Typical first visit flow:**

1. Enter building, find **ticket machine** (*seiriken*).
2. Select **moving-in registration** (*tennyu*).
3. Wait until your number appears on screen.
4. Submit forms, residence card, lease proof at window.
5. Staff may send you to **insurance** or **pension** desk with a new ticket.

**Example:** You arrive in Setagaya on a Tuesday at 10 a.m. Wait 35 minutes. Counter takes 15 minutes. Insurance desk adds another 20 minute wait. You leave tired but done. That is success.

## Residence registration explained

**Jumin toroku** ties your name to an address in that municipality.

| Task | When |
| --- | --- |
| Moving in (*tennyu*) | Within legal deadline after arrival or move |
| Moving out (*tenshutsu*) | Before leaving a municipality |
| Address change | Every apartment change |

| Document (common) | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Residence card | ID and visa status |
| Passport | Backup ID |
| Lease or housing proof | Address verification |
| Completed municipal form | Download PDF before you go |

Photograph every **stamped copy** before you leave the building.

## My Number and official paperwork

| Step | Where it often happens |
| --- | --- |
| Receive **My Number notification** | Mail or ward counter |
| Apply for **Individual Number Card** | Ward office or online |
| Pick up card and set PIN | Appointment often required |
| Update address on record | Ward office after move |

Guide: [My Number Card for foreign residents](/residents/japan-my-number-card).

Stamp (*hanko*) culture exists but many foreigners use signature. Ask staff what they accept for your form.

## National health insurance registration

**Kokuho** is usually handled at the municipal desk, often right after registration.

| Step | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Submit enrollment form | Linked to your address record |
| Insurance card timing | Same day or by mail |
| Premium notices | Arrive later based on income |

If you are on **employee insurance** through work, your employer enrolls you. City hall is still relevant for address and certificates.

Guide: [National Health Insurance for residents](/residents/japan-health-insurance).

## Pension enrollment confusion

| Track | Who handles it |
| --- | --- |
| **Employees' pension** | Employer payroll |
| **National pension** | Students, freelancers, gaps between jobs |

After registration you may get papers at the counter or by mail. Do not throw them away because the kanji looks scary.

Guide: [Japan pension system](/residents/japan-pension-system).

<InfoBox title="Most foreign residents misunderstand this">
  - **"Pension is optional because I am foreign."** Many residents must enroll or apply for a lawful exemption.
  - **"My company said they handle everything."** Confirm you are actually on employees' pension.
  - **"I can ignore the blue envelope."** Deadlines are real. File or ask at the counter.
</InfoBox>

## Address changes and moving procedures

When you move **within Japan**:

1. **Moving-out** at old municipality (or combined flow per city rules).
2. **Moving-in** at new municipality within the deadline.
3. Update **bank, employer, phone**, and My Number address.

| Mistake | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Skip moving-out | Mail and tax records split wrong |
| Register at wrong ward | Visit rejected, day wasted |
| Use hotel as permanent address | Usually not valid long term |

**Quick answer:** Two wards means two sets of bills if you forget moving-out. Fix it early.

## Resident tax and official notices

City hall does not invent resident tax alone, but your **municipal record** drives bills and questions.

| Notice type | Why it scares newcomers |
| --- | --- |
| Resident tax slip | Japanese only, June timing |
| Insurance premium change | Income reassessment |
| Voting registration | Unfamiliar envelope |

Open mail promptly. Pay or ask at the tax counter. Context: [Japanese taxes](/residents/japan-taxes-guide), [pay bills in Japan](/residents/pay-bills-japan).

## Language barrier realities

| Reality | What helps |
| --- | --- |
| Full English rare outside big wards | Foreign resident desk if available |
| Fast spoken Japanese | Ask staff to write key words |
| Form kanji fields | Pre-fill at home from PDF |
| Translation apps | Good for words, risky for legal sentences |

| English phrase | Japanese (polite) |
| --- | --- |
| Moving in registration | Tennyu todoke onegaishimasu |
| Moving out | Tenshutsu todoke onegaishimasu |
| National health insurance | Kokumin kenko hoken |
| Residence certificate | Juminhyo |
| Please write it down | Kaite kudasai |

Staff are often patient when you are prepared and calm. Perfect Japanese is not required.

## Number systems, counters, and waiting culture

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |

| 2 | Take printed **ticket number** |
| 3 | Wait in seating area |
| 4 | Watch screen or listen for your number |

## FAQ

### What is the difference between city hall and ward office?

City hall serves a city. Ward offices serve wards inside large cities like Tokyo. Both handle resident registration for their area.

### Do foreign residents have to visit city hall?

Yes for registration and most address changes. Some certificate tasks move to konbini with My Number Card later.

### How soon after moving must I register?

Deadlines apply, commonly within 14 days of moving. Confirm on your municipality website.

### Is city hall the same as immigration?

No. Immigration is national. City hall is local registration and municipal services.

### Can I register using a hotel address?

Usually not as a long-term registered address. Use your legal residence.

### Do I need an appointment?

Depends on ward. Walk-in works in many places. Popular Tokyo wards offer online slots.

### Will they keep my residence card?

They inspect and return it in normal cases.

### What if I do not speak Japanese?

Use a foreign resident desk in big wards, bring a friend, or ask staff to write instructions.

### How long is the wait?

Certificate only off-peak: often 10-30 minutes. Registration plus insurance on Monday morning: 45-90+ minutes possible.

### Where do I pay resident tax?

Often by mailed slip or employer withholding. City hall tax desk answers questions.

### What documents do I need for a residence certificate?

Residence card and fee. Some wards ask for My Number. Same-day pickup is common.

### Does moving change my My Number?

The number stays. Update your address record. Card update may be a separate step.

### Can both spouses register together?

Often one person can submit if documents are complete. Couples sometimes visit together for clarity.

### What if I miss my ticket number?

Get a new ticket. Politely explain at the help desk if your ward has one.
