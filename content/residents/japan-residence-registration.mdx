## Why residence registration matters in Japan

Your registered address is how Japan knows where to send insurance bills, tax notices, and election paperwork. Banks, phone carriers, and employers often ask for proof tied to that record.

- Without registration, many services stall or require extra visits.
- Your residence card and municipal record should show the same address.
- Moving apartments means updating registration, not only changing mail.
- Short gaps between temporary and permanent housing still need a clear plan.

<InfoBox title="Watch for this">
  A hotel address usually cannot serve as your long-term registered address. Plan a lease, share house contract, or other acceptable housing before your registration appointment when possible.
</InfoBox>

## What happens after arriving in Japan

Most mid- to long-term residents receive a residence card at the airport or immigration office, then register where they live within the required window.

- Land, open a phone if needed, and secure a bed you can document.
- Visit the ward office or city hall that covers your address.
- Complete moving-in registration and related enrollments in one trip when staff allow.
- Keep every stamped paper in one folder labeled with dates.

<TipBox title="Field note">
  Photograph your residence card and registration receipt the same day. If you lose the card later, copies speed up replacement steps.
</TipBox>

## What a ward office or city hall does

In Tokyo you usually visit the **ward office** (区役所). Other cities use **city hall** (市役所) or town village offices. They maintain the resident registry for your address.

- Issue or update your residence record (juminhyo when you need copies later).
- Route you to health insurance and pension desks in the same building on many visits.
- Handle My Number notification cards and related mail.
- Answer procedural questions when you bring Japanese forms and ID.

## Documents you usually need

Requirements vary by municipality. Arrive with originals and a simple folder. Staff rarely accept blurry phone photos as substitutes.

### Residence card

Your **residence card** (在留カード) shows status, period of stay, and later your registered address.

- Bring the physical card every visit until staff say otherwise.
- Report address changes on the card when you move.
- If the card is pending, bring passport plus any immigration notice you received.

### Passport

Keep your **passport** available for identity checks, especially on first registration.

- Valid passport matches the name on leases and cards.
- Carry a copy in cloud storage, not only the physical book.

### Address information

Staff need a concrete address they can file, not a vague neighborhood.

- Rental contract, management company letter, or landlord contact details.
- Building name in Japanese, room number, and postal code.
- Host or employer support letter only if your city accepts it for your case.

## Registering your address

**Moving-in registration** (転入届) tells the city you now live there. This is the step people mean when they say register your address.

- Fill the moving-in form with Japanese address blocks; clerks can check spelling.
- State prior address in Japan or overseas as the form requests.
- Ask which enrollments you can complete the same day.
- Receive insurance and pension guidance sheets before you leave.

## My Number basics

**My Number** (マイナンバー) is a personal ID number used for tax and social systems. You receive a notification card by mail after registration.

- The number itself is not a daily wallet card for most people.
- My Number card (plastic IC) is optional and requires a separate application.
- Do not post photos of notification cards on social media.
- Store the number like a tax ID: share only with trusted institutions.

<InfoBox>
  Card pickup appointments and photo rules change. Read the latest municipal leaflet rather than old forum threads.
</InfoBox>

## National Health Insurance enrollment

Most residents without employer health coverage enroll in **National Health Insurance (NHI)** through the city after address registration.

- Premiums depend on prior-year income reported to the municipality.
- You receive a health insurance card by mail after enrollment.
- Clinics ask for the card at check-in.
- See the dedicated NHI guide for fever visits, copays, and workplace switches.

## Pension enrollment overview

Pension runs parallel to health insurance. Students, employees, and freelancers land in different tracks.

- Employees usually join through payroll after HR onboarding.
- Many non-employed residents start with **kokumin nenkin** and pay notices by mail.
- Reduction forms exist for students and low-income cases if you apply.
- Use the pension guide for payment rhythm, exemptions, and departure refunds.

## Updating your address when moving

When you change apartments, file **moving-out** at the old city and **moving-in** at the new one within the required period.

- Update your residence card address at immigration or online if eligible.
- Re-enroll insurance and pension through the new municipality.
- Forward mail with Japan Post after dates are official.
- Tell your bank, employer, and phone carrier once the city confirms the new record.

<TipBox>
  Book two half-days if you move across ward lines in Tokyo: one visit to close the old record, one to open the new.
</TipBox>

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Waiting weeks to register while using only a hotel address
- Assuming employer HR completed city registration for you
- Ignoring Japanese mail because the kanji looks routine
- Forgetting to update the residence card after a move
- Losing the only copy of a lease before the ward appointment

## What to expect at Japanese city halls

City halls reward preparation more than charm. Queues move when your forms are complete.

- Take a number ticket at the entrance and wait for your screen to flash.
- Speak slowly, use short sentences, or bring a friend for Japanese if needed.
- Morning slots are often quieter than lunch rush.
- Staff may send you to a second window for insurance after registration finishes.

| Situation | Practical approach |
| --- | --- |
| Form only in Japanese | Ask for a sample copy or point to your passport name spelling |
| Missing lease page | Call the management company from the waiting area |
| Child or spouse visa | Bring family cards and marriage documents if staff request them |
| No appointment system | Arrive early with documents already sorted |

## Simple first-week checklist for new residents

- Secure housing you can document on a lease or accepted letter
- Register moving-in at the ward or city office
- Apply for or confirm health insurance and pension path
- Save My Number mail when it arrives
- Photograph residence card front and back
- Set mail access so municipal envelopes are not lost

## Final advice

Residence registration is bureaucratic but finite. One organized visit prevents a month of catch-up calls.

Treat the ward office as the source of truth for your address. When forums disagree with your city’s PDF, trust the PDF on your kitchen table.
