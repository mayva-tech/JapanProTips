## Quick answer: Which side do people stand on?

| Region | Typical standing side | Walking lane |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tokyo and most Kanto** | **Left** | Right |
| **Osaka and much of Kansai** | **Right** | Left |
| **Kyoto, Nagoya, others** | Often follow local norm | Watch signs and locals |

When unsure: **stand on the side most people use**, keep bags in front, leave one lane clear if walkers exist.

## Why escalator rules matter in Japan

Japan values **smooth public flow**. Escalators are narrow choke points.

| Why it matters | Station reality |
| --- | --- |
| Rush hour volume | Hundreds per hour on one staircase |
| Predictability | Commuters move without talking |
| Safety | Falls cause serious delays |
| Tourist luggage | Wide bags block lanes fast |

You are not failing culture if you adjust slowly. You are joining a **moving system**.

## Regional comparison table

| City / region | Typical standing side | Walking side |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tokyo** | Left | Right |
| **Osaka** | Right | Left |
| **Kyoto** | Often right (Kansai influence) | Opposite standing side |
| **Nagoya** | Mixed; often left-stand like Tokyo | Check local habit |
| **Hokkaido / Tohoku** | Often left-stand | Similar to Tokyo in cities |
| **Fukuoka** | Kansai-leaning in places | Observe first ride |

Rules are **habits**, not laws. Signs sometimes override habit.

## Tokyo vs Osaka escalator differences

| Topic | Tokyo habit | Osaka habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing | Left | Right |
| Walking commuters | Pass on right | Pass on left |
| Tourist shock | High when arriving Osaka | High when returning Tokyo |
| Signage | Some stations say "do not walk" | Same safety campaigns |

**Example:** Day one Tokyo, you learn left. Day six Osaka, you step left by reflex. Someone says *sumimasen* behind you. You switch. You laugh later. Everyone has been new once.

## Train station crowd flow culture

Stations are choreography: gates, stairs, escalators, platforms.

| Flow element | Etiquette |
| --- | --- |
| **Escalator** | Stand one side, walk other (if walking allowed) |
| **Stairs** | Often walk any side; still keep right passing space |
| **Platforms** | Line up behind marks; let off first |
| **Doors** | Remove backpack in crowds |

## The unspoken walking lane system

Where walking is accepted, one side stands, one side moves.

| Standing traveler | Walking commuter |
| --- | --- |
| Stay on standing side | Use walking lane |
| Hold bag in front | Do not stop mid-escalator |
| Kids close to adult | No phone zombie drift |

<InfoBox title="Most tourists misunderstand this">
  - **"There is a national rule."** There is a **regional habit** plus station policy.
  - **"Everyone must walk."** Many people stand. Some stations discourage walking.
  - **"Locals will yell at you."** Usually a polite pass or subtle *excuse me* energy.
  - **"Tokyo rules work everywhere."** Osaka will humble you gently.
</InfoBox>

| Rush hour truth | Tourist impact |
| --- | --- |
| Fast walkers | Standing in walking lane feels stressful |
| Packed steps | No room to correct side mid-ride |
| Suitcases | Enemy of flow |
| Delay ripple | One fall stops a line |

**Quick answer:** At peak hour, **stand, hold bags tight, do not stop at top**.

## Why some stations discourage walking

Safety campaigns appear in major hubs: **stand still**, hold handrail, no running.

| Reason | Sign you may see |
| --- | --- |
| Fall injuries | Pictograms, PA announcements |
| Aging society | Stability over speed |
| Narrow escalators | Less safe passing space |

If signs say do not walk, **everyone stands**. Walking lane culture pauses.

## Escalator etiquette tourists should know

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Watch one ride before stepping on | Stopping at landing to check phone |
| Stand on local standing side | Standing in the middle |
| Keep suitcase behind you, narrow | Sideways mega-bag block |
| Hold handrail if tall or tired | Leaning on wrong side rail awkwardly |
| Step off quickly at top | Group huddle at exit |

## FAQ

### Which side do you stand on escalators in Tokyo?

Typically **left**. Walkers use the right when walking is allowed.

### Which side in Osaka?

Typically **right**. Walkers use the left.

### Is walking on escalators allowed?

Often yes in commuter culture, but **many stations discourage it** for safety.

### What if I stand on the wrong side?

Move when you notice. Locals are usually patient with obvious tourists.

### Can I take suitcases on escalators?

Yes, but keep them narrow and behind you. Use elevators when available.

### Do Kyoto escalators follow Tokyo or Osaka?

Often **Kansai-style right stand**, but observe locals.

### Why do Tokyo and Osaka differ?

Regional habit, not official national law. Debates continue domestically too.

### Will people get angry?

Usually not. You may hear a soft pass request behind you.

### Are there signs about escalator rules?

Sometimes **stand still** safety posters override walking culture.

### What should I do during rush hour?

Stand still, one correct side, bags contained, exit quickly at top.
