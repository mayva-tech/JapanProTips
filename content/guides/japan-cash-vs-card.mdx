## Is Japan still a cash society?

**Partly, but less than before.** Daily life in cities moved toward cards, IC payments, and mobile pay. Many small owners and rural shops still prefer **yen notes**.

Think in layers:

| Layer | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **IC card (Suica, etc.)** | Trains, many stores, vending |
| **Credit / debit card** | Hotels, chains, big restaurants |
| **Cash** | Shrines, tiny shops, some izakaya, tips not needed |
| **Mobile pay** | Bonus if your wallet supports it |

You need **more than one method**, not a suitcase of cash only.

## The reality in 2026

| Trend | Tourist impact |
| --- | --- |
| **Card acceptance up** | Urban dining and retail mostly OK |
| **Contactless common** | Tap at konbini and stations |
| **IC everywhere** | Default for transport + small spend |
| **Cash not dead** | Backup still required |
| **No tipping culture** | Simpler math at table |

Old blog posts saying "Japan is 100% cash" mislead you. Old posts saying "cards everywhere like the US" also mislead you.

## Where cards work almost everywhere

| Place | Card odds |
| --- | --- |
| **Hotels** | Very high |
| **Department stores** | Very high |
| **Major restaurant chains** | High |
| **Konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart)** | High |
| **Airport and station retail** | High |
| **Museums and attractions** | High (some cash desks) |
| **Don Quijote, drugstores** | High |
| **Shinkansen reserved seats online** | Card at purchase |

Use a **no foreign transaction fee** Visa or Mastercard. Amex works in fewer small shops.

## Where cash is still commonly needed

| Place | Why cash |
| --- | --- |
| **Small ramen and izakaya** | Owner choice, machine may be cash |
| **Shrines and temple offerings** | Coin boxes |
| **Farm stands, rural cafes** | No terminal |
| **Some bars and old shops** | Legacy habit |
| **Lockers (old banks)** | Coins or IC only |
| **Tiny guesthouses** | Sometimes bank transfer or cash |

<InfoBox title="Tourists panic about this">
  One cash-only dinner does not mean your card failed forever. Carry **¥5,000-¥10,000** backup and you are fine in cities.
</InfoBox>

## IC cards explained

**IC cards** are stored-value tap cards for transport and payments.

| Card | Region |
| --- | --- |
| **Suica** | Tokyo / JR East (tourist default) |
| **PASMO** | Tokyo private lines |
| **ICOCA** | Kansai |
| **Others** | Interoperable nationwide for transit and many shops |

They are **not credit cards**. You load yen, then tap.

Deep dive: [Suica and Pasmo guide](/guides/suica-pasmo-guide).

## Suica and PASMO for daily spending

Tourists use IC for:

- **Trains and buses**
- **Konbini** purchases
- **Vending machines**
- **Some restaurants and shops** with IC mark
- **Coin lockers** (smart types)

| Method | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| **Physical Suica** | Airport buy, easy |
| **Mobile Suica (iPhone)** | Top up in app |
| **Welcome Suica** | Visitor card, expires |

**Example day:** ¥800 train taps, ¥600 konbini lunch, ¥200 drink machine = **¥1,600** off IC without opening wallet.

Reload at station machines or konbini when balance under **¥1,000**.

## Mobile payments in Japan

| System | Tourist use |
| --- | --- |
| **Apple Pay / Google Pay** | Works if card issuer supports Japan contactless |
| **Suica in Apple Wallet** | Excellent for visitors with compatible iPhone |
| **PayPay, LINE Pay** | Usually need Japanese bank; skip unless you have local account |
| **QR at shops** | Increasing; card-linked wallets easier than local QR apps |

Mobile pay is a **convenience layer**, not a replacement for cash backup.

## ATM realities for tourists

Best tourist ATMs:

| Location | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **7-Eleven ATMs** | Wide foreign card support |
| **Japan Post Bank ATMs** | Good hours in post offices |
| **Airport** | Rates OK for first withdrawal |

| Tip | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Withdraw **larger amounts** fewer times | Per-fee savings |
| Use **debit with low FX fee** | Better than airport only |
| Notify bank before travel | Avoid blocks |
| **PIN** must be 4 digits for many ATMs |

**Example:** Withdraw **¥30,000** once, carry **¥10,000** in wallet, rest in hotel safe. Top up IC separately.

## Convenience stores and international cards

Konbini are a **card acceptance benchmark**.

- Credit tap at register: common
- IC tap: common
- Cash: always works

Also: ATMs, bill pay, ticket machines. See [convenience store guide](/guides/japan-convenience-store-guide).

If your card fails at konbini, call issuer. If it works there, you are probably fine in cities.

## Restaurants and cash-only surprises

| Sign | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Cash only** (*genkin only*) | Plan yen |
| **Pay machine at door** | May be cash or IC only |
| **Counter ramen** | Often cash or IC |
| **High-end dinner** | Usually card |

**Practical scenario:** Famous ramen line. Machine shows cash and IC only, no Visa. You tap Suica. Crisis avoided because you loaded IC.

## Rural Japan payment realities

| Rural | City |
| --- | --- |
| More cash | More card |
| Fewer ATMs | ATMs every block |
| Family restaurants cash | Chains card |
| Bus exact change sometimes | IC bus tap |

Hokkaido drives, mountain towns, and island trips: carry **extra ¥10,000-¥20,000** buffer.

## How much cash tourists should carry

| Trip style | Daily cash on person |
| --- | --- |
| **Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka only** | ¥5,000-¥10,000 |
| **Mixed urban + rural** | ¥10,000-¥15,000 |
| **Heavy rural / festival** | ¥15,000-¥20,000 |

Total trip cash need is lower than a decade ago if you use **card + IC**.

| Length | Extra ATM plan |
| --- | --- |
| **7 days** | 1-2 withdrawals |
| **14 days** | 2-3 withdrawals |

## Exchange rates and withdrawal strategy

| Method | Typical verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **ATM withdrawal (7-Eleven)** | Often best for tourists |
| **Home bank FX order** | OK if rate good |
| **Airport exchange counter** | Convenient, worse rate |
| **Hotel exchange** | Usually worst |

Compare your bank's **ATM fee + FX spread** vs airport.

Withdraw yen. Pay in **yen** when asked on card terminal (decline dynamic currency conversion to USD).

## Common payment mistakes tourists make

- **Only bringing cash** and skipping IC setup
- **Only bringing one card** that fails everywhere
- **Empty IC** before last train
- **Dynamic currency conversion** in USD on terminal
- **No PIN** on debit card
- Assuming **PayPay** works without Japanese setup
- **Not carrying small bills** for shrines and lockers

## FAQ

### Can I use only credit card in Japan?

Urban trip only: almost, but risky. Always carry some cash and IC.

### How much yen should I bring from home?

Bring **¥0-¥20,000** from home if your currency exchange is poor. ATM in Japan is fine.

### Does Japan accept contactless?

Yes widely at chains and konbini. Rural varies.

### Is Suica the same as a credit card?

No. Prepaid yen balance. Load before spend.

### Which ATM for foreign cards?

7-Eleven and Japan Post Bank are reliable.

### Do I need PayPay?

No for short trips unless you already have it set up.

### Are 1000 yen coins important?

Yes for lockers, shrines, small shops.

### Is cash dirty or disappearing?

Still respected. Not disappearing, but less dominant in cities.

### What if my card is declined?

Try second card, use ATM cash, check bank travel notice.
