## Why tourists use coin lockers in Japan

Common situations:

- **Checkout before flight or train** and hours to kill
- **Day trip** from Tokyo to Kamakura with a base hotel
- **Rainy hour** without dragging suitcases through shops
- **Temple town** walking where wheels bounce on stone

Lockers are cheaper than dragging bags and faster than shipping for **same-day, same-city** storage.

## Where coin lockers are usually found

| Location | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Major JR stations** | Tokyo, Shinjuku, Kyoto, Osaka, Nagoya |
| **Subway hubs** | Often near main exits |
| **Airports** | Narita, Haneda, Kansai (limited hours) |
| **Shopping buildings** | Department stores, some malls |
| **Tourist areas** | Asakusa, Hakone bus terminals |
| **Konbini** | Some FamilyMart locations (regional) |

Look for signs: **コインロッカー**, **ロッカー**, or pictograms of a box with key.

**Practical scenario:** You check out of a Shinjuku hotel at 10 a.m. Store one carry-on and two daypacks at Shinjuku Station, lunch in Harajuku, collect bags at 4 p.m. before Narita Express.

## Types of lockers explained

Sizes vary by operator. Labels are often **S / M / L** or dimensions in cm.

### Small

- **Fits:** Daypack, single shoulder bag, small shopping
- **Typical size mindset:** up to about 40 cm tall
- **Use:** Coats, purchases, light hands-free sightseeing

### Medium

- **Fits:** Cabin-size roller, two medium backpacks
- **Typical size mindset:** about 40-55 cm tall
- **Use:** Most common tourist sweet spot

### Large

- **Fits:** Full-size checked suitcase (sometimes tight)
- **Typical size mindset:** about 55-84 cm tall
- **Use:** One big bag; scarce at peak times

### Smart lockers

- **Touch panel** in Japanese + sometimes English
- **IC card** or QR payment common
- **Digital receipt** with pickup code or card tap again
- Often at renovated station banks

<InfoBox title="Tourists panic about this">
  Your **29-inch checked bag** may not fit a "large" locker at an older bank. Test the door before you pay. Have a Plan B (forwarding or another station wing).
</InfoBox>

| Size | Rough fit | Tourist use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small | Backpack | Shopping runs |
| Medium | Cabin roller | Day trips |
| Large | Full suitcase | Checkout day (if available) |
| Smart | Same sizes, digital UI | IC payment, receipts |

## Typical pricing and time limits

Prices vary by city and operator. Treat numbers as **ranges**, not guarantees.

| Size | Typical price range (24h) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small | ¥300-¥500 | Cheapest |
| Medium | ¥500-¥700 | Most used |
| Large | ¥700-¥900+ | Premium when available |

| Time rule | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **24-hour blocks** | Pay for first day; extra fee after |
| **Overdue** | Higher rate per extra day; do not ignore |
| **Max days** | Often 3-7 days; read panel |
| **Clear by midnight** | Some old lockers; check screen |

Always read the **end time** on the receipt or screen before you walk away.

## How payment works

### Coins

- Older lockers take **¥100 coins** only
- Coin change machines nearby (accept bills, give coins)
- Carry coins if you use rural or old banks

### IC cards

- **Suica / Pasmo / ICOCA** work on many smart lockers
- Tap to lock, tap to unlock
- Same card must return: [Suica and Pasmo guide](/guides/suica-pasmo-guide)

### QR systems

- Some banks use **credit card + QR receipt** on phone
- Screenshot the code; phone battery matters at pickup

Payment habits: [money and payments in Japan](/guides/money-payments-japan).

## Step-by-step usage guide

**Smart locker (IC card) example:**

1. Find an **empty** locker (green light or open door).
2. Open door, place bag inside.
3. Close door firmly until latch clicks.
4. On panel: choose size if prompted, select **IC card payment**.
5. Tap card. Wait for confirmation sound.
6. Note **location number** (row A-12, etc.) on screen or photo it.
7. Return with same card, tap reader, door unlocks.

**Coin locker example:**

1. Open empty locker.
2. Put bag in, close door.
3. Insert required coins until lever unlocks or key releases.
4. **Take the key** (key stays with you) or remember built-in combo.
5. Return within time limit; insert key or coins if extra day fee applies.

<TipBox title="Station tip">
  Photo the **locker number**, **station exit name**, and **wing** (Yaesu vs Marunouchi). Stations are large. "Tokyo Station locker" is not one place.
</TipBox>

## What happens if lockers are full

| Option | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Another bank** | Same station, different floor or exit |
| **Nearby station** | One stop away may have space |
| **Luggage office** | Paid staffed storage (higher cost, reliable) |
| **Hotel hold** | Ask front desk if you are guest |
| **Forward luggage** | [Luggage shipping](/guides/japan-luggage-shipping) to next hotel |

Weekends, cherry blossom, and New Year: expect **full large lockers** at Tokyo and Kyoto.

## Oversized luggage realities

| Item | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **28-30 inch suitcase** | May need largest class; not always available |
| **Two suitcases** | Usually need **two lockers** (cost doubles) |
| **Bulky duffel** | Soft bags fit better than hard shells |
| **Stroller** | Some stations have dedicated storage |

If bags are huge, **forwarding** often beats locker roulette.

## Tokyo station locker chaos explained

Tokyo Station is a **city inside a city**. Locker banks sit at multiple exits.

| Area name | Tourist note |
| --- | --- |
| **Yaesu side** | Popular, fills fast |
| **Marunouchi side** | Another bank, worth checking |
| **Gransta / underground** | Smart lockers, signage in English sometimes |
| **JR vs subway** | Different operators, different banks |

Arrive **before 10 a.m.** on busy days if you need large slots. Avoid assuming one empty light means the whole station is open.

## Shinkansen travelers and luggage strategy

Shinkansen platforms reward **light boarding**.

| Strategy | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Locker at **departure station** morning | Sightsee, return for train |
| **Forward** big bag to next hotel | Ride with daypack only |
| Use **overhead rack** for medium bag | Limited space; no aisle blocking |

Rules and tips: [Shinkansen guide](/guides/shinkansen-guide) and [Japan trains](/guides/japan-trains).

**Example:** Kyoto to Osaka day trip from Tokyo base is awkward with two large bags. Locker in Tokyo OR forward one bag to Osaka hotel.

## Coin lockers vs luggage forwarding

| Factor | Coin locker | Luggage forwarding (Yamato) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Same day, same city** | **Best** | Overkill |
| **City to city next day** | Awkward if you travel | **Best** |
| **Cost (one bag, one day)** | ¥500-¥900 typical | ¥1,500-¥2,500+ typical |
| **Large suitcase** | Hard at peak | Reliable |
| **Hands-free Shinkansen** | Good if locker near platform | **Better** multi-city |

Use lockers for **hours**. Use forwarding for **days and distances**.

## Safety and theft realities

Japan lockers are **generally safe**. Theft from station lockers is rare but not impossible.

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Do not store **passport only** without backup plan | Loss is painful |
| Keep **wallet and phone** on you | Obvious but common mistake |
| Use **staffed storage** for high-value gear if nervous | Peace of mind |
| Check **pickup time** | Abandoned bags get handled by station rules |

Do not store **illegal items** or perishables that smell.

## What tourists are surprised by

<InfoBox title="Tourists are surprised by">
  - Lockers exist **inside and outside** ticket gates (know which side you need)
  - **IC tap** is easier than hunting ¥100 coins
  - Large slots sell out while small ones sit empty
  - You must **retrieve before max days** or staff intervention follows
  - Some lockers are **narrow depth**: wide bag fails even if tall fits
  - **Airport lockers** cost more but solve last-day tourism
</InfoBox>

## Common mistakes visitors make

- Paying for **medium** when bag needed **large**
- Storing **outside** ticket gate then needing **inside** platform access
- Forgetting **which exit wing** the locker was in
- Missing **overdue fees** and finding locker cleared
- Putting **food** that smells in a closed box all day
- Assuming partner has the **IC card** that locked the door
- **Blocking** locker rows while repacking (step aside)

## Best locker strategies during busy seasons

1. **Early morning** drop before crowds.
2. Carry **exact change** as backup at old banks.
3. Know **two locker banks** at your station on Google Maps.
4. Size up: if unsure, choose **larger** class.
5. For golden week or sakura peak, book **forwarding** instead of hoping.
6. Travel with **cabin-size** main bag when possible.
7. Screenshot locker **number and payment receipt**.

## What happens if you forget your code or key

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Forgot IC card** | Same physical card usually required; get card before panic |
| **Lost key (coin locker)** | Find station **information desk** or locker company number on bank |
| **Dead phone (QR)** | Charge phone or ask staff with receipt ID |
| **Overdue** | Pay extra fee shown on panel or at desk |
| **Abandoned long term** | Items moved per station policy; reclaim at office with ID |

<InfoBox title="Tourists panic about this">
  Staff help **every day**. Bring passport, describe locker number and payment time. You are unlikely to be the first tourist that day.
</InfoBox>

## FAQ

### Do coin lockers still take only coins?

Many new banks use **IC cards or cards**. Older banks still want ¥100 coins. Read the panel before you load the bag.

### Can I use Suica for lockers?

Yes at many smart locker banks. Tap same card to open.

### How long can I leave luggage?

Often **24-72 hours** per payment tier. Max total days posted on machine.

### Are lockers inside the ticket gate?

Both exist. Remember if you must **enter or exit** the gate to reach your locker.

### Can I store a suitcase at Tokyo Station overnight?

Often yes for 1-3 days if you pay overdue rules. Large slots are the bottleneck, not the clock.

### Is it cheaper than luggage forwarding?

For **same-day storage**, usually yes. For **intercity moves**, forwarding is often better value.

### What if all lockers are full?

Try another bank, nearby station, staffed storage, or forward the bag.

### Are lockers safe for laptops?

Generally yes in Japan. Risk is low; your comfort level may prefer staffed storage.

### Do airports have lockers?

Yes at major airports. Prices and size limits vary. Hours may match terminal operations.

### Can two people share one locker?

If it fits and closes, technically yes. Payment is per locker, not per person.
