## Quick answer: Why is my take-home pay smaller?

**Gross salary (face amount) is not what lands in your bank account.** Employers withhold social insurance and income tax. **Resident tax** often hits harder from year two.

| You see in offer letter | What hits your account |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly gross (額面) | Minus insurance and tax |
| Annual bonus promise | Separate timing, taxed |
| Overtime potential | May be limited or structured |

Rough rule for many single employees in cities: **take-home is often around 75-85% of monthly gross** before major life changes. Your case varies.

## Why first paychecks shock foreign residents

| Shock moment | Why it happens |
| --- | --- |
| Offer letter looked big | Letter shows gross |
| Deduction lines in Japanese | Hard to verify first month |
| Resident tax not explained | May appear later as bills |
| Bonus expectations | Culture differs by industry |
| Overtime assumptions | Contracts vary |

**Example:** Gross ¥300,000. Bank deposit ¥245,000. Rent is ¥85,000. Suddenly "good salary" feels tight. That is math, not failure.

## Gross salary vs take-home salary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Gross (総支給)** | Salary before deductions |
| **Deductions (控除)** | Insurance, tax, other |
| **Net / take-home (手取り)** | What you receive |

| Who quotes what | Tip |
| --- | --- |
| Recruiters | Often gross monthly |
| Job ads | Usually gross annual |
| Bank deposit | Always net |

Budget from **net**, not gross.

Living costs: [cost of living in Japan](/residents/japan-living-cost). Savings habits: [budgeting in Japan](/residents/japan-savings-budgeting-guide).

## Salary deduction table

| Deduction type | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Health insurance** | National or employee health insurance premiums |
| **Pension** | Kokumin or employees' pension contributions |
| **Employment insurance** | Unemployment insurance (雇用保険) |
| **Income tax** | National income tax withholding |
| **Resident tax** | Local inhabitant tax (may be withheld later) |
| **Other** | Company mutual aid, union fees, dorm charges if any |

<InfoBox title="Most foreign residents misunderstand this">
  - **"My contract says ¥X so I get ¥X."** Contract is usually gross.
  - **"Taxes are the same as my home country."** Japan mix is its own pattern.
  - **"Resident tax is tiny."** It can be a full month of salary spread across the year.
  - **"Bonus is extra free money."** It is taxed income, not guaranteed in all jobs.
</InfoBox>

## Resident tax surprise in second year

**Resident tax (住民税, inhabitant tax)** funds your city and prefecture. Many newcomers miss it early.

| Resident tax timing | Typical experience |
| --- | --- |
| **First calendar year in Japan** | Often low or no salary withholding for resident tax |
| **Second year onward** | Bills or payroll withholding based on prior income |
| **Long-term resident** | Stable monthly withholding or municipal invoices |

| Why year two hurts | Feeling |
| --- | --- |
| Prior year income assessed | Higher bill |
| Withholding starts on payslip | Take-home drops suddenly |
| June bills (common pattern) | "Where did this come from?" |

**Quick answer:** Resident tax is often roughly **roughly 10% of prior-year income**, spread across months. Exact amount is municipal.

Pay bills on time: [paying bills in Japan](/residents/pay-bills-japan).

## Bonus culture in Japan

| Bonus reality | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Summer / winter bonus** | Common in large companies, not universal |
| **ALT / contract roles** | May have no bonus |
| **Startups** | Variable or none |
| **Taxation** | Bonuses taxed like salary |

Do not budget rent assuming bonus until you see two cycles paid.

## Payday culture and timing

| Pattern | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Monthly pay** | Often 20th or 25th, company-specific |
| **Bank transfer** | Same day nationwide common |
| **Payslip (給与明細)** | PDF or paper; keep copies |
| **Year-end adjustment** | December-January paperwork |

Set rent auto-debit **after** payday date you learn.

## Contract salary vs actual take-home pay

| Contract says | You should plan on |
| --- | --- |
| ¥4,000,000 annual gross | Monthly gross ÷ 12, then deduct |

## FAQ

### Why is my first paycheck so low in Japan?

Deductions for insurance and income tax start immediately. Gross was never the deposit amount.

### What is resident tax in Japan?

Local tax based on prior-year income. Often noticeable from the second year.

### Is bonus guaranteed in Japan?

No. Depends on company, role, and contract.

### How often do people get paid in Japan?

Usually monthly. Payday date is company-specific.

### Can I reduce pension payments?

Some exemptions exist for low income or students. Rules are strict. See pension guide.

### What percentage is taken from salary in Japan?

Varies widely. Many employees see **15-25%** total withholdings before resident tax changes, not a fixed rule.

### Do part-time workers get the same deductions?

Often yes on payroll, with hour and visa limits separate.

### Where does salary go?

Bank account registered with employer. Confirm account details at onboarding.

### Should I do year-end tax adjustment?

If employed, HR usually guides **nenmatsu chousei**. Submit forms on time.

### Is salary enough to live in Tokyo?

Depends on net, rent, and family size. See living cost guide for ranges.
