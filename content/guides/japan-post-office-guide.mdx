## Why tourists end up using Japanese post offices

Common triggers:

- **Postcards** bought at temples, stations, and museum shops
- **Souvenir boxes** that exceed airline weight limits
- **Gifts** for family back home
- **Documents** that must go by post, not email
- **Last-day errands** when only a post office can handle international customs forms

Japan Post is not the only option for parcels. It is the default for **letters, postcards, and many international small parcels**.

## What Japan Post actually is

**Japan Post** (日本郵便, *Nihon Yūbin*) runs the national postal network. Tourists usually see:

| Brand / sign | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **JP (ゆう)** red logo | Post office and postal counters |
| **ゆうパック (Yu-Pack)** | Domestic parcel service |
| **EMS** | Fast international express mail |
| **International parcel post** | Air or surface by weight and zone |

Post offices range from large branches with English help to small neighborhood counters inside a supermarket building. Hours vary. Many close weekends or early evenings. Check Google Maps for **郵便局** (*yūbinkyoku*) near your hotel.

## Post office vs convenience store shipping

| Need | Post office | Convenience store (konbini) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Postcards and stamps | **Best choice** | Some sell stamps; limited help |
| International customs forms | **Yes** | Usually no full international post desk |
| Small domestic parcel (Yu-Pack) | Yes | Often via **Yamato** or similar, not Japan Post |
| Luggage hotel to hotel | Rare at post office | **Yamato counter** common |
| Late night | Limited | Konbini hours longer |

<TipBox title="Quick tip">
  Think **post office = mail and official international post**. Think **konbini = courier pickups and many domestic parcel labels**, often Yamato. See [convenience stores in Japan](/guides/japan-convenience-store-guide).
</TipBox>

## Mailing postcards in Japan

**Domestic postcards** (within Japan):

1. Buy blank or picture postcards at shops, or at the post office.
2. Write the address on the **right side** (Japanese format: postal code, prefecture, city, street, name).
3. Leave the **back left** for your message if the card is divided.
4. Affix the correct **postage stamp** (rate board at post office; domestic postcards are a fixed rate, often around **¥85**, confirm on site).
5. Drop in a **post box** (赤いポスト) or hand to counter.

**International postcards** (to your home country):

1. Address in **English** (or local language) with country name in **capital letters** last line.
2. Buy **international postcard rate** stamps or pay at counter.
3. Staff may ask you to leave the card **unsealed** for inspection if rules apply.

**Example scenario:** Ten postcards from Kyoto to the US. Buy international stamps at Kyoto central post office, write addresses at the hotel, drop all ten in the post box before checkout. Allow **1-3 weeks** for surface paths, less for air.

## Buying stamps explained

| Stamp type | Tourist use |
| --- | --- |
| **Fixed-value postcard stamps** | Domestic or international postcard rate |
| **Sheet stamps** by yen value | Letters and odd weights |
| **Commemorative sheets** | Souvenirs; still valid postage |

- Stamps are sold at post office counters and some **konbini**.
- You can overpay slightly; underpay and mail is returned.
- Staff can calculate postage if you show the item at the counter.

<InfoBox title="Tourists are surprised by">
  You rarely lick modern Japanese stamps. Many are peel-and-stick. Counters also print **metered postage** on the spot for parcels.
</InfoBox>

## Domestic shipping services

**Yu-Pack** is the main domestic parcel brand.

| Factor | Typical tourist notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Size** | Small box of snacks to medium suitcase box |
| **Speed** | Often next day or day after within region |
| **Tracking** | Yes, with receipt |
| **Where to send** | Hotel (confirm with hotel first), friend in Japan, Airbnb |

**Example:** Send a box of ceramics from Kanazawa to your Tokyo hotel arriving the day after you do. Hotel holds it at front desk if you emailed arrival date.

Domestic hotel forwarding by **courier** is often easier for large luggage: [luggage delivery in Japan](/guides/japan-luggage-shipping).

## International shipping services

Options at the post office counter (names may show in English at large branches):

| Service | Speed | Cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EMS** | Fastest (days to many countries) | Highest | Urgent gifts, valuable items |
| **Air parcel / small packet** | Medium | Mid | Snacks, books, clothes |
| **Surface (sea mail)** | Slow (weeks to months) | Lowest | Heavy non-urgent boxes |

Staff weigh the box, ask contents, and help with **customs declaration** (CN22/CN23 style forms).

**Example scenario:** 4 kg box of candy and towels to Germany. Air small packet if budget matters less than time; surface if you already flew home and can wait.

Declare contents **honestly**. Food and alcohol rules depend on destination country.

## Sending luggage and souvenirs home

| Item type | Practical path |
| --- | --- |
| Postcards | Stamps + post box |
| Small souvenirs in flat box | International parcel post or EMS |
| Full suitcase home | Often **airport cargo**, airline baggage, or **Yamato** to airport; compare [luggage shipping guide](/guides/japan-luggage-shipping) |
| Heavy ceramics | EMS or air parcel; pack well with bubble wrap sold at post office |

Post offices sell **boxes, tape, and padding**. Pack fragile items with space for shaking.

## Yamato vs Japan Post differences

| | **Japan Post** | **Yamato (Kuroneko)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strength** | Mail, postcards, international post forms | Hotel to hotel luggage, konbini drop-off |
| **Tourist postcard** | **Primary choice** | Not for standard postcards |
| **Large suitcase between hotels** | Possible but less common workflow | **Very common** |
| **Where** | Post office | Hotels, airports, Lawson, FamilyMart, etc. |
| **Tracking** | Yu-Pack / EMS numbers | Kuroneko tracking number |

Many trips use **both**: Yamato for bags between cities, Japan Post for postcards and one box home.

## How addresses work in Japan

Japanese addresses read **large to small**:

1. **Postal code** (7 digits, format 123-4567)
2. **Prefecture** (都道府県)
3. **City / ward** (市区町村)
4. **Town / chome / block** (町名・丁目・番地)
5. **Building name and room** (if any)
6. **Recipient name**

For mail **to Japan**, get the hotel's full address from the front desk card, often with Japanese and English.

For mail **from Japan abroad**, use your home address in your language plus **country in block capitals**.

Deeper guide (also useful for tourists): [how Japanese addresses work](/residents/japan-address-system).

## How to fill out shipping forms

At the counter, staff often help. If you fill forms yourself:

1. **Sender** block: your name, Japan hotel address, phone if asked.
2. **Recipient** block: full home address, phone, email sometimes.
3. **Contents**: list items plainly (e.g. "chocolate, T-shirt, books").
4. **Value**: declare approximate value in yen for customs.
5. **Sign** where indicated.

<TipBox title="Practical scenario">
  You are shipping tea and snacks. Write "green tea bags, rice crackers" not "food stuff." Customs likes specificity.
</TipBox>

Keep the **receipt and tracking slip** until delivery is confirmed.

## Language barrier realities

- Large city post offices (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka stations) often have **English-capable** staff at international counters.
- Neighborhood branches may be **Japanese only**. Show the package and say "international" or "kaigai."
- Point to a **photo of your country flag** on rate charts if needed.
- Translation app for address characters helps.
- **Patience and politeness** go far. Staff are used to helping visitors.

Useful romanized words:

| Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Kaigai yūbin | International mail |
| Hagaki | Postcard |
| Kitte | Stamp |
| Konpo | Parcel |
| EMS | EMS (same) |

| Method | Typical at post office |
| --- | --- |
| **Cash (yen)** | Always accepted |
| **Credit / debit cards** | Common at larger branches |
| **IC cards / mobile pay** | Increasing at some counters; do not rely on it everywhere |

Carry **cash** as backup. See [money and payments in Japan](/guides/money-payments-japan).

## Delivery timing expectations

| Mail type | Rough expectation (varies by country) |
| --- | --- |
| Domestic postcard | A few days |
| International postcard (air) | About 1-2 weeks |
| EMS international | Often **3-7 days** to many regions |
| Air parcel | About 1-3 weeks |
| Surface sea mail | **1-3 months** |

Typhoons, holidays, and customs in your home country add delay. Ship **before your last full day** if you need counter help.

## Common mistakes tourists make

- **Wrong postage** on postcards (returned or delayed)
- **Forgetting country name** in capitals on last line abroad
- **Sealing** international postcards when counter asked them open
- **Packing prohibited items** (batteries, aerosols, fresh meat: check rules)
- **Undeclared value** on customs forms
- **Assuming hotel receives parcels** without notice
- **Waiting until airport** for international parcel post (airport has services, but city post office is calmer for first-timers)
- Using **Yamato** when they needed **stamps** for postcards

More general trip friction: [tourist mistakes in Japan](/guides/japan-tourist-mistakes).

## What tourists are surprised by

<InfoBox title="Tourists are surprised by">
  - Post offices feel **quiet and bank-like**, not chaotic.
  - Staff may **repack** your box to meet size tiers and save you money.
  - **Post boxes** are everywhere, including remote shrine roads.
  - **EMS** is fast but not cheap; surface mail is very slow but real.
  - You can buy **nice stamp sheets** as affordable souvenirs that still mail.
  - Konbini **does not replace** every post office task for international mail.
</InfoBox>

## Useful phrases and tips

| English | Japanese (polite) | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| International mail, please | Kaigai yūbin onegaishimasu | At counter |
| Postcard stamps to [country] | [Kuni] made no hagaki kitte kudasai | Buying stamps |
| How much is postage? | Ryōkin wa ikura desu ka? | Any item |
| Track this package | Kono tōtatsu bangō o kakunin shitai desu | After shipping |
| Do you have a box? | Konpō bako wa arimasu ka? | Packing |

**Tips:**

- Photograph your **tracking number** and receipt.
- Visit **before 4 p.m.** at smaller branches to avoid rush close.
- Ask hotel to **print your home address** if handwriting is messy.
- Keep **one afternoon buffer** for shipping day.

## FAQ

### Can tourists use Japan Post?

Yes. No residence card required for standard mail and tourist parcels.

### Where do I mail postcards in Japan?

Any post box with the red JP mark, or the post office counter for stamps and questions.

### How much is a postcard stamp in Japan?

Domestic and international rates differ. Check the rate table at the post office; staff will tell you the total for your destination.

### Is EMS worth it for souvenirs?

Worth it for **time-sensitive or valuable** items. For heavy low-value goods, compare air parcel or surface.

### Can I ship luggage with Japan Post?

Small and medium boxes yes. Full suitcase workflows are often easier with **Yamato** or airport services.

### Do hotels accept packages for guests?

Many do if you **notify them** with tracking number and arrival date. Confirm before shipping.

### Can I pay with card at the post office?

Often yes at larger branches. Carry yen anyway.

### What cannot be mailed internationally?

Varies by country. Common issues: fresh food, plants, liquids, lithium batteries in some configurations. Ask staff.

### Post office or konbini for a box to my country?

**Post office** for standard international postal forms. Konbini is better for many **domestic courier** flows.

### How early should I ship before flying home?

At least **2-3 business days** before departure for counter service; more for surface mail if you leave Japan before it arrives.
