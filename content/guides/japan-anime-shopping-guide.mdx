## Why anime shopping in Japan overwhelms tourists

Common overload triggers:

- **Too many floors** in one building
- **Japanese-only** labels on limited goods
- **Same character** in 12 product types
- **Tax-free rules** that exclude some items
- **Size and weight** of figures in luggage
- **FOMO** on seasonal exclusives

A simple plan beats wandering: pick **one district per half-day**, one **must-buy category**, and a **budget cap**.

## Best anime shopping areas

### Akihabara (Tokyo)

- **Electric town** plus anime flagship density
- New goods, games, cranes, maid cafes (optional)
- Best for: **first visit energy**, major chains, retro game alleys
- Crowded weekends; go weekday morning for calmer aisles

### Ikebukuro (Tokyo)

- **Otome Road** and female-targeted anime goods strong
- Huge **Animate** flagship, Pokémon Center nearby
- Best for: **BL, idols, character cafes**, modern retail

### Nakano Broadway (Tokyo)

- Indoor mall of **used and collector** shops
- **Mandarake** complex dominates
- Best for: **vintage, rare, doujin, secondhand figures**

### Den Den Town (Osaka)

- Osaka's **Akihabara-style** strip in Nipponbashi
- Slightly more compact, strong retro game presence
- Best for: **Kansai base** without Tokyo day trip

| District | Vibe | Best for | Crowd |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Akihabara | Neon, dense | First-timers, games, cranes | High |
| Ikebukuro | Modern mall anime | Animate, Pokémon, otome | High |
| Nakano Broadway | Collector indoor | Used, rare, vintage | Medium |
| Den Den Town | Osaka compact | Kansai shoppers | Medium |

## Types of stores explained

### Animate

- **New official** goods: merch, CDs, books, exclusives
- Tax-free often available (check counter)
- Floor guides by series; staff polite, busy

### Mandarake

- **Secondhand** center: figures, manga, games, cosplay
- Condition grades vary; inspect box dents
- Nakano flagship is legendary; multiple Tokyo branches

### Surugaya

- **New and used** mix; strong game and manga sections
- Price tags clear; multiple branches in Akihabara

### Book Off

- **Used books, games, manga** at discount
- Hit or miss inventory; great budget digging
- Not anime-only but frequent scores

### Don Quijote

- Discount **merch wall**, snacks, cosplay accessories
- Tax-free desks in tourist areas
- Impulse zone: fun, not deepest collector stock

| Store | New | Used | Figures | Games | Tax-free often |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Animate | Yes | Rare | Yes | Some | Yes |
| Mandarake | Some | Yes | Yes | Yes | Varies |
| Surugaya | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Varies |
| Book Off | No | Yes | Sometimes | Yes | Varies |
| Don Quijote | Yes | Some | Some | Some | Yes |

## New vs secondhand anime goods

| | **New** | **Secondhand** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price** | MSRP + Japan exclusives | Often 30-70% off |
| **Condition** | Sealed, mint | Check box and paint |
| **Rare items** | Lottery and pre-order | Vintage finds |
| **Tax-free** | Easier at big retailers | Smaller shops vary |
| **Emotional** | "I bought it in Japan" sealed | "Treasure hunt" win |

<TipBox title="Practical collector tip">
  Hunt **one grail used** at Mandarake first. Buy **new exclusives** you cannot get home at Animate second. Saves budget and bag space.
</TipBox>

## Figure shopping explained

**Scale figures** (1/7, /8, Nendoroid, Pop Up Parade) dominate tourist bags.

| Type | Price mindset | Bag risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nendoroid** | ¥5,000-¥8,000+ | Small box, safer |
| **Pop Up Parade** | ¥4,000-¥6,000+ | Medium |
| **Scale 1/7** | ¥15,000-¥30,000+ | Large, heavy |
| **Prize figures** | ¥2,000-¥4,000 | Won from crane; bulky |

- Buy from **official retailers** for authenticity
- Check **box art** and hologram stickers when present
- **Window boxes** may have shelf wear even when figure is new

**Example:** One Nendoroid + two prize figures ≈ ¥15,000-¥20,000 and fits carry-on. One scale figure may need **shipping home**.

## Crane games and arcades

**UFO catchers** stack plush and figures behind glass.

| Reality | Tip |
| --- | --- |
| Harder than videos suggest | Budget ¥500-¥2,000 play money |
| Staff **can help** (politely ask) | Common in Akihabara |
| Won items are **large** | Plan bag space |
| Not cheaper than buying | Entertainment + occasional win |

Late-night arcade energy overlaps with [nightlife in Japan](/guides/japan-nightlife-guide) districts, but shops are separate venues.

## Retro game stores

Akihabara and Den Den Town excel at **Famicom, PS1, GB cartridges, arcade boards**.

| Buy | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Cartridge games | Pins, label, boot |
| Consoles | Region (NTSC-J), power |
| Handhelds | Battery swelling on old units |
| Disc games | Scratches, case cracks |

Surugaya and specialist shops label **used condition**. Book Off is lottery digging.

## Manga shopping realities

| Format | Tourist note |
| --- | --- |
| **Japanese tankobon** | Cheap per volume; heavy in bulk |
| **English manga** | Available in big cities; less "Japan only" |
| **Doujinshi** | Adult/fan works; some floors 18+ |
| **Magazines** | Thick, disposable weight |

<InfoBox title="Tourists overbuy this">
  Ten manga volumes you could order at home later. Buy **1-2 art books** or **Japan-only** volumes instead unless shipping a box.
</InfoBox>

## Tax-free shopping for anime goods

Short-term tourists can skip **10% consumption tax** on eligible purchases at participating shops.

| Rule | Anime shopper impact |
| --- | --- |
| **Passport required** | Physical passport usually |
| **Minimum spend** | Often ¥5,000+ per store/day rules |
| **Consumables** | Snacks sealed until exit |
| **Figures and goods** | Usually eligible at major chains |
| **Used shops** | Ask; not always offered |

## Pricing expectations

Rough retail mindsets (new, pre-tax display common):

| Item | Typical range |
| --- | --- |
| Keychain, small acrylic | ¥500-¥1,500 |
| Towel, plush small | ¥2,000-¥4,000 |
| Nendoroid | ¥5,500-¥7,500+ |
| Scale figure | ¥15,000-¥35,000+ |
| Art book | ¥3,000-¥5,000 |
| Used manga volume | ¥200-¥500 |
| Retro game cart | ¥500-¥5,000+ rare high |

**Example day budget:** ¥10,000-¥25,000 for mixed small goods + one mid figure. Easy to double if scales appear.

## Counterfeit concerns

| Safe habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Buy at **Animate, official stores, major chains** | Licensed stock |
| Avoid **absurdly cheap** "scale" on street stalls | Bootlegs exist |
| Check **sculpt quality** and box printing | Blurry photos on box |
| Mandarake grades **used** honestly | Still inspect |

If price looks too good for a ¥25,000 figure, walk away.

## Limited edition and seasonal releases

- **Collab cafes** and pop-up shops rotate monthly
- **Gachapon** (capsule toys) at station machines: ¥200-¥500
- **Lottery figures** (*ichiban kuji*): buy tickets for prize tiers
- **Seasonal** summer/winter character goods sell out fast

You cannot buy everything. Pick **one series** and commit.

## Etiquette in anime stores

- **Ask before opening** display boxes unless shelf says sample OK
- **Queue** at tax-free counters
- **Photography** signs vary; respect no-photo stickers
- **Adult floors** are 18+; do not bring kids through
- **Quiet voice** in Mandarake narrow aisles
- **Do not block** crane machines during someone else's turn

## FAQ

### Is Akihabara still worth it?

Yes for first-timers. Add Nakano if you like used goods.

### Can I get tax-free on figures?

Usually at major chains with tax-free desk. Bring passport.

### Are used figures safe to buy?

Inspect at Mandarake. Grades help. Popular tourist path.

### How many figures can I fit in a suitcase?

2-4 small boxes or 1 large scale depending on case. Measure at hotel.

### Is manga in English sold?

Yes in big cities. Japanese volumes are cheaper locally.

### What is Mandarake?

Large secondhand anime, manga, game, and figure chain. Nakano Broadway main hub.

### Can I ship figures internationally?

Yes via Japan Post EMS or couriers. Declare value honestly.

### Are crane game prizes authentic?

Usually yes from arcades. Quality tier below retail figures.

### Is Den Den Town worth a day trip from Kyoto?

If you are in Osaka/Kyoto area, yes. Tokyo shoppers focus on Akihabara/Nakano.

### Do stores close early?

Many by 8-9 p.m. Arcades later. Plan tax-free before closing.
