## Quick answer: Best ATMs for tourists

**Start with 7-Eleven (Seven Bank).** Backup with **Japan Post (JP Bank / Yucho)**. Test your card on day one, not on day five in a rural town.

| Priority | ATM |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **7-Eleven** (Seven Bank) |
| 2 | **Japan Post** (ゆうちょ / JP Bank) |
| 3 | Lawson or FamilyMart (card-dependent) |
| Avoid for tourists | Random local bank ATMs in small towns |

Payments context: [money in Japan](/guides/money-payments-japan). Card vs cash rules: [cash vs card](/guides/japan-cash-vs-card).

## Why cash still matters in Japan

Japan moved toward cards and QR pay. Tourist life still touches **cash-only friction**.

| Situation | Cash role |
| --- | --- |
| Small restaurants | Cash only or cash preferred |
| Shrines and donations | Coins and bills |
| Some ryokan or rural inns | Cash settlement |
| Ticket machines | Card sometimes fails |
| Lockers and small shops | Coins and ¥1,000 notes |
| Markets and stalls | Cash common |

<OperationalWarning noteId="cash-withdrawal-rural-timing">
  <p>
    First withdrawal should happen where Seven Bank or Japan Post is a short walk,
    not on day five when you are rural and the lone ATM is closed. Test PIN and
    limit flags early while you still have options.
  </p>
</OperationalWarning>

**Example:** You find a perfect ramen shop seats six people. Sign says cash only. You have ¥800 in coins and a card you cannot use. That is a real Tuesday in Kyoto, not a 1990s postcard.

## ATM comparison for tourists

| ATM type | Foreign card reliability | Typical availability | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **7-Eleven ATM** | **High** for many Visa/Mastercard debit cards | Urban 24/7, widespread | Default tourist recommendation |
| **Japan Post ATM** | **High** backup | Post offices, some stations | Hours vary; not always 24/7 |
| **Lawson ATM** | Medium | Dense in cities | Works for many cards, not universal |

## The reality of foreign cards in Japan

Your card works at home. Japan ATMs are a **different network conversation**.

| Factor | What tourists feel |
| --- | --- |
| **Debit vs credit** | Debit cash advance rules differ |
| **Bank flags** | First Japan withdrawal triggers fraud block |
| **Daily limits** | ¥50,000-¥100,000 caps common |
| **Chip + PIN** | PIN required on many machines |
| **Weekend timing** | Home bank fraud desk closed |

<WhatPeopleMiss noteId="cash-withdrawal-two-networks">
  <p>
    One card is a single point of failure. Two cards on different networks is
    boring insurance that saves dinner when Visa works but Mastercard does not, or
    the reverse, at a stubborn machine.
  </p>
</WhatPeopleMiss>

<InfoBox title="Most tourists misunderstand this">
  - **"Japan is cashless now."** Tokyo is card-heavy. Small towns and old-school shops are not.
  - **"One failed ATM means my card is broken."** Try Seven Bank before panic.
  - **"Credit card = ATM cash."** Cash advance fees and interest hurt.
  - **"Airport exchange is the same as ATM."** Rates and fees differ; ATM is often fine if your bank is fair.
</InfoBox>

**Quick answer:** Notify your bank before travel, carry **two cards** from different networks if possible, and test withdrawal early.

## 7-Eleven ATM guide

Seven Bank ATMs inside **7-Eleven** are the standard tourist answer.

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Find 7-Eleven on Maps |
| 2 | Choose **English** on screen |
| 3 | Insert card chip-first |
| 4 | Select withdrawal, enter PIN |
| 5 | Choose **yen (JPY)** amount |
| 6 | Decline DCC if asked in home currency |

| Tip | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Withdraw daytime first trip | Confirms card works |
| Take photo of decline code | Helps bank support |
| Keep receipt | Track fees and rate |

Airport arrival: [first steps at the airport](/guides/japan-airport-first-steps). SIM for maps: [SIM guide](/guides/sim-card-japan).

## Japan Post ATM guide

**Japan Post Bank (Yucho)** ATMs are the best backup when 7-Eleven fails or is far.

| Pro | Con |
| --- | --- |
| Strong foreign card support | Post office hours |
| Found in towns 7-Eleven skips | Some machines older UI |
| Often in stations | Lines at peak |

Search **JP Bank ATM** or post office with ATM sign. English available on many screens.

## Lawson ATM guide

Lawson ATMs work for **many** tourists, not all.

| When Lawson helps | When to skip |
| --- | --- |
| Lawson is closest open store | You have not tested card yet |
| 7-Eleven out of service | Rural Lawson with limited ATM |
| Late-night urban stop | First withdrawal of trip |

Treat Lawson as **Plan B**, not Plan A.

**Real scene:** You try three blue bank ATMs. All decline. You walk seven minutes to 7-Eleven. It works. You feel foolish for not going there first.

## Understanding withdrawal fees

Fees stack in layers tourists do not see until home.

| Possible fee type | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Local ATM fee** | Japan-side charge, often ¥0-¥220 on tourist-friendly ATMs |
| **Home bank fee** | Flat $3-5 or "international ATM" fee per withdrawal |
| **Exchange rate markup** | Bank converts at rate worse than mid-market |
| **Dynamic currency conversion** | ATM offers charge in USD/EUR with bad rate |

**Quick answer:** One larger withdrawal often beats five tiny ones if your home bank charges per transaction.

Budget planning: [Japan trip cost breakdown](/guides/japan-budget-breakdown).

## Dynamic currency conversion trap

The screen asks: **"Charge in your home currency?"**

| Choice | Result |
| --- | --- |
| **Charge in JPY (yen)** | Usually correct |
| **Charge in home currency (DCC)** | Often worse rate, hidden markup |

Always select **yen** unless you have a rare reason not to.

**Example:** Screen shows $120 USD or ¥18,000. Pick yen. Let your home bank convert later.

| Sign clue | Meaning |

## How much cash tourists should realistically carry

| Situation | Recommended cash |
| --- | --- |
| **Tokyo day trip** | ¥5,000-¥10,000 buffer |
| **Rural travel** | ¥15,000-¥30,000 for 2-3 days |
| **Small restaurants** | ¥3,000-¥5,000 per meal cushion |
| **Train travel day** | ¥2,000 coins + ¥10,000 notes |
| **Temple visits** | ¥500-¥1,000 coins for donations |
| **Full card-heavy itinerary** | Still keep ¥10,000 minimum |

Adjust for your bank fees: one ¥30,000 withdrawal vs three ¥10,000 ones.

## FAQ

### What is the best ATM in Japan for foreigners?

**7-Eleven Seven Bank** is the usual first choice. **Japan Post** is the best backup.

### Can I use my debit card at Japan ATMs?

Often yes for Visa and Mastercard debit. Notify your bank and know your PIN.

### Do Japan ATMs charge fees?

Some do. Your home bank may also charge per withdrawal. Read your account terms.

### Should I withdraw yen at the airport?

Yes for peace of mind if rates are acceptable. Confirm your card works before leaving the terminal area.

### Why did my card work yesterday but not today?

Fraud blocks, daily limits, damaged chip, or wrong ATM network. Try Seven Bank.

### Is Japan Post ATM open on weekends?

Many post office ATMs have limited hours. 7-Eleven is better for late night.

### How much cash should I carry per day in Tokyo?

Many travelers do fine with **¥5,000-¥10,000** cash buffer plus cards.

### Are Japanese banks ATMs okay for tourists?

Often **no** for foreign cards. Use Seven Bank or Japan Post.

### What is dynamic currency conversion?

ATM offers billing in your home currency with a poor exchange rate. Choose **yen** instead.

### Can I pay trains with cash only?

IC cards and cards cover most urban trains. Rural lines and some machines still favor cash.
