## Why 100 yen shops become addictive in Japan

| Reason | Resident effect |
| --- | --- |
| **One-stop household** | Solve small problems same day |
| **Dense urban life** | Tiny problems multiply |
| **Seasonal rotation** | New reasons to visit monthly |
| **Low regret price** | Try before big purchase |
| **Near stations** | Errand on commute home |

You do not shop because you love plastic. You shop because **your apartment has no junk drawer yet**.

**Foreign residents misunderstand this:** Treating 100 yen shops as toy stores. Locals use them as **hardware, organization, and seasonal supply** shops.

**Apartment example:** Move-in Saturday. ¥3,000 at Seria: hooks, bath mat, dish rack, extension cord sleeve, labels. Kitchen works Monday. No furniture store trip needed.

## FAQ

### Are Daiso, Seria, and Can Do the same?

Similar idea. Different design and SKU mix. Residents often prefer one.

### Is everything 100 yen?

No. Check tags. Many items ¥200-¥500.

### Which chain is best?

Daiso for range. Seria for calm organization. Can Do for character seasonals.

### Can I furnish an apartment only from 100 yen shops?

Basics yes. Bed, desk, sofa need other sources.

### Tax included?

Shelf shows 税込 at most chains now.

### Best first purchase list?

Hooks, bath mat, laundry clips, food containers, cleaning sponges.

### Do shops take card?

Usually yes in cities.

### Are products safe for food?

Use labeled kitchen silicone and containers.

### How often do seasons change?

Major rotations roughly quarterly plus monthly highlights.
