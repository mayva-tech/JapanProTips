## Why job hunting in Japan feels different

| Difference | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| **Formal applications** | Rirekisho, stamps, handwriting culture in some firms |
| **Slow replies** | Weeks of silence is common, not always rejection |
| **Group hiring seasons** | New grads peak; mid-career hires year-round but structured |
| **Indirect communication** | "We will consider" may mean no |
| **Visa sponsorship filter** | Many roles never open to foreigners |
| **Appearance norms** | Suit, neat hair, on-time arrival matter early |

Treat the process as a **system**, not a single perfect resume blast.

## Part-time vs full-time job culture

| | **Part-time (baito)** | **Full-time (seishain)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hours** | Limited weekly cap on student visas | Standard full schedule |
| **Contract** | Hourly, flexible shifts | Salary, bonuses, benefits |
| **Japanese need** | Basic often enough in hospitality | Higher for most office roles |
| **Visa** | Must match permitted activities | Employer sponsors or valid work visa |
| **Growth** | Income bridge, language practice | Career track, shakai hoken |

Deep dive on baito roles: [part-time jobs in Japan](/residents/part-time-jobs-japan).

## Japanese language expectations realistically explained

| Level (rough guide) | What you can often do |
| --- | --- |
| **N5-N4** | Konbini, warehouse, some kitchens with training |
| **N3** | Service jobs, simple office support |
| **N2** | Many Japanese office environments |
| **N1** | Client-facing Japanese business, keigo-heavy roles |
| **No JLPT but fluent conversation** | Still hireable if interview proves skill |

Employers care about **workplace Japanese**, not only test scores.

<InfoBox title="Foreign residents misunderstand this">
  **"English-only jobs"** still expect basic Japanese for HR, contracts, and daily life. The job post language is not the whole workplace language.
</InfoBox>

## Resume systems in Japan

Japan uses two main documents. Many applications ask for both.

### Rirekisho (履歴書)

Standard **personal history form**: photo, education timeline, licenses, reason for leaving prior jobs, family section, handwriting in traditional companies.

| Field | Tip |
| --- | --- |
| **Photo** | Professional studio headshot, neutral background |
| **Date submitted** | Write current date when you send |
| **Reason for application** | Short, positive, company-specific |
| **Commute time** | Estimate door to door |
| **Stamp** | Some forms still want personal seal (inkan) |

Buy blank forms at convenience stores or use online templates (Rirekisho Maker, etc.).

### Shokumukeirekisho (職務経歴書)

**Career history sheet** for mid-career and skilled roles: projects, metrics, tools, leadership.

| Section | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Summary | 3-5 lines on specialty |
| Work blocks | Company, dates, role, achievements with numbers |
| Skills | Languages, software, certifications |
| Self PR | What you want next and why this company |

### Resume comparison

| | **Rirekisho** | **Shokumukeirekisho** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Format** | Fixed template | Free layout |
| **Length** | 1-2 pages | 2-4 pages common |
| **Photo** | Required | Usually no |
| **Best for** | First jobs, traditional firms | Mid-career, IT, consulting |
| **Language** | Japanese standard | Japanese; English appendix sometimes OK |
| **Handwriting** | Sometimes requested | Typed accepted more often |

Keep a **Japanese master** and an **English backup** for international HR.

## Interviews and etiquette

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Document screening** | Weeks possible |
| **First interview** | HR or manager, often 45-60 min |
| **Second+** | Team meet, case study, technical test |
| **Final** | Executive, conditions discussion |

**Example flow (IT mid-career):**

1. Online application + shokumukeirekisho
2. HR phone screen in Japanese (30 min)
3. Technical interview with team lead
4. Final with department head and HR offer terms

**Example flow (restaurant baito):**

1. Walk-in or LINE application
2. Short in-person chat same week
3. Trial shift

Bring **printed copies**, pen, resume, residence card copy only if asked, and questions written down.

## Why appearance and punctuality matter

Japan interviews still weigh **first impression** heavily.

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Dark suit, white shirt, conservative tie or blouse | Loud perfume, casual sneakers |
| Neat hair and nails | Sloppy bag or wrinkled clothes |
| Arrive 10 minutes early | Even 2 minutes late without call |
| Quiet phone off | Checking phone in waiting area |
| Bow greeting, polite keigo attempts | Over-casual slang with unknown age hierarchy |

Being foreign does not remove these norms in traditional companies.

## Foreign-friendly companies vs traditional companies

| | **Foreign-friendly** | **Traditional Japanese** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Language** | English internal docs possible | Japanese default |
| **Hiring** | Portfolio and skills weighted | University name, tenure pattern |
| **HR** | Explains visa steps | Expects you to know rules |
| **Culture** | Direct feedback | Indirect, harmony-first |
| **Examples** | Global tech, some startups, inbound tourism | Banks, manufacturers, many SMEs |

Read **Glassdoor-style reviews** and ask current employees on LinkedIn before you commit.

## Recruiters and dispatch companies explained

| Type | How it works | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recruitment agency (人材紹介)** | Introduces you to client companies | Fee paid by employer; never pay illegal placement fees |
| **Dispatch (派遣)** | You are employed by agency, assigned to client | Lower stability, different benefits |
| **Temp-to-perm (紹介予定派遣)** | Trial period then direct hire | Contract terms in writing |

Good agents help with **visa paperwork** for eligible roles. Weak agents spam unsuitable jobs.

## Visa and work restrictions basics

Your **residence card** lists status and work permission.

| Status (examples) | Work note |
| --- | --- |
| **Engineer/Specialist in Humanities** | Employer tied; change needs notification or permission |
| **Student** | 28 hours/week cap (stricter during breaks rules) |
| **Dependent** | Permission to engage in activity required |
| **Permanent resident / Spouse** | Broader work allowed |
| **Designated activities** | Read card footnotes carefully |

Unauthorized work risks **visa cancellation**. Employer should file updates when you change jobs if required.

## Common mistakes foreign residents make

- Applying only in English to Japanese-only HR
- One generic rirekisho for every company
- Bad or casual **ID photo**
- Talking salary too early in first interview at traditional firms
- Ignoring **28-hour rule** on student visas
- Assuming silence means ghosting (follow up once politely)
- Not researching **commute time** they will ask about
- Oversharing negative stories about past bosses

## What employers actually care about

| Priority | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Visa eligibility** | Legal hire possible |
| **Reliability** | Show up on time, finish training |
| **Team fit** | Low conflict, learns rules |
| **Japanese for the floor** | Customers and coworkers |

Enthusiasm matters, but **trust** matters more.

## FAQ

### Can I work full-time on a student visa?

No for standard student status beyond permitted part-time hours with approval.

### Do I need JLPT on my resume?

Helpful proof, not always required if you demonstrate Japanese in interview.

### Should I handwrite rirekisho?

Some traditional companies prefer it. Tech firms often accept typed forms. Ask HR.

### How long does hiring take?

Part-time: days to 2 weeks. Full-time corporate: 1-3 months common.

### Is it okay to negotiate salary?

More common in IT and foreign firms. Traditional first interview: wait for offer stage.

### What if I only speak English?

Target global teams, teaching, tourism, and inbound roles. Learn survival Japanese anyway.

### Can I work two part-time jobs?

Visa permitting total hours, yes. Watch schedule overlap and tax paperwork.

### Do I need a Japanese bank account before applying?

Not to apply. Often before first paycheck.

### Are dispatch jobs bad?

Not always. Read contract, benefits, and conversion path.

### What documents after offer?

Residence card, My Number, bank book, pension enrollment forms, emergency contact.
